## Description
Tonight, if your tooth starts to throb, you'll take a painkiller and book a dentist. Fourteen thousand years ago, a young man in the Italian Alps had the same pain — and his dentist had a rock.

In this video, we explore what ancient humans actually did about toothache: the first dental operations, the strangest fillings ever found, a worm that doctors blamed for thousands of years, and the one bad decision that made our teeth worse.

In this video:
- Why toothache hurts so much (and why it could kill)
- Neanderthal toothpicks and the 14,000-year-old flint "drill"
- The oldest filling on Earth: tar, plant fibres… and hair
- 9,000-year-old bow-drill dentistry in Pakistan
- The tooth worm, frog mouthwash and the first toothpaste
- George Washington's teeth and "Waterloo teeth"
- The twist: what ancient plaque DNA reveals about sugar
- How a dentist gave the world pain-free surgery

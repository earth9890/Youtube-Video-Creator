## Checked and NOT used
- **Pope et al. 2000 (Am J Psychiatry), "men think women want more muscle":** per the press release, men picked an ideal body far more muscular than their own and believed women wanted more muscle than women in fact chose. A strong fact, but it adds a fourth study and frames the topic around attracting women. Saved for a future episode. https://www.newswise.com/articles/womens-ideal-man-is-less-muscular-than-men-think
- **Kleck & Strenta 1980 "scar" study** (fake facial scar secretly wiped off; volunteers still felt judged): vivid, but I could not reach the original paper to verify the details, and secondary retellings vary. Not used.
- **Hubal variability caveat:** the training was one arm, 12 weeks, young adults (18-40); the size range (-2 % to +59 %) shows that "hardgainers" are partly real, which is why the script admits "thirty-six arms barely grew" before the reversal. The script promises strength, not size.
- **Exact G.I. Joe Extreme figure:** quoted as 26 in (TIME), 26.8 in or "27 in" elsewhere. The script gives no number, only "bigger than the biggest bodybuilders" (the paper's own abstract wording).
- **Episode overlap:** the Barry Manilow study was noted but cut in ep14 (not used on screen); ep21 used a different Gilovich study (Deri, Davidai & Gilovich 2017). Ep13's facts (fat leaves as CO2, fat-cell number) are not touched.
- **Diet, calorie surplus, protein or supplement advice:** deliberately left out (series rule: no diet or supplement selling). Hubal's volunteers were in fact excluded if they took protein or muscle-building supplements, and kept their normal diet.

## Checked and NOT used
- **"80 % of elevator door-close buttons don't work":** traced to a 2014 Radiolab interview with an elevator-museum curator; no data behind it (computer.rip analysis above). Not used.
- **"Door-close buttons have been dead since 1990":** an overstatement of Penafiel's NYT quote. The button cannot shorten the ADA minimum, works in fire service, and in some buildings works after the minimum. The script says exactly that.
- **"90 % of office thermostats are dummies":** a forum recollection of a Wall Street Journal story; no source found. The WSJ piece itself (Jared Sandberg, Jan 2003) could not be read. Not used.
- **Edinburgh** (~50-60 of ~300 junctions show the green man automatically) and **Manchester** (~40 % of buttons don't need pressing at busy times), BBC 2013: true, cut for length.
- **Henlys Corner, London:** since 2012 the crossing runs automatically on the Sabbath, after talks with the Jewish community (BBC 2013). Lovely, cut for length.
- **Sydney:** CBD crossings already cycled automatically 7 am-7 pm; from 23 March 2020 they did so 24 hours to avoid touching buttons (Government News, 24 Mar 2020: https://governmentnews.com.au/sydney-traffic-lights-automated-to-combat-COVID-19). Cut for length.
- **Melbourne CBD automatic from 5:30 am to after midnight** (Victoria Walks): advocacy-group claim, not checked against VicRoads. Not used.
- **TfL "Green Man Authority"** (green by default, red only when a vehicle is detected; 20 more crossings from Sept 2020, ianVisits): cut for length.
- **NYC 2004: 750 working locations** (Luo). Not voiced; the script uses the 2016 figure of 120 to show the current trend.
- **Langer & Rodin 1976 nursing-home plants study:** famous perceived-control study, but its long-term mortality follow-up is small and debated; not needed.
- **Geer, Davison & Gatchel 1970** (false belief that reaction time could shorten shocks reduced arousal): supports the twist, cut for length.
- **Ellen Langer's quote "Perceived control is very important… It diminishes stress and promotes well being."** (NYT 2016): cited above as support; cut from the voiced script for length.
- **Title direction "Why Most Buttons Don't Do Anything":** not true as stated (most buttons work); the chosen title avoids the claim.

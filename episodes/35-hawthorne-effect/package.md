## Description
When your boss walks past, do you suddenly type faster? Psychologists call it the Hawthorne effect, and it started in a giant telephone factory near Chicago a hundred years ago.

In 1924, engineers at Western Electric's Hawthorne Works tried to prove that better lighting makes workers faster. Output went up when they turned the lights up, went up in the group whose lights never changed, and kept going up as the lights went down towards moonlight. Then five women building telephone relays got a room of their own, a paper tape that counted every relay, an observer, rest breaks and the occasional tongue sandwich, and the legend of "being watched" was born. Hand washing in a hospital hallway, an honesty box under a printed pair of eyes: the effect seemed to be everywhere. Until the missing data turned up on old microfilm.

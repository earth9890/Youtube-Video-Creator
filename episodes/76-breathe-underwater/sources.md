# Episode 76 "What If You Could Breathe Underwater?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, Tom, Poppy, Mister Pike, the barrel and the lake are story devices. Everything the narrator or Doc states as fact is below. **Solid** = published source. **Inference** = our arithmetic from solid numbers. Quotes are exact as returned by the page.

| "Your gills have collapsed and stuck together, like wet tissue paper." / Brain: "Gills need water to hold their shape." | Solid. Out of water, gill filaments collapse and clump. | "Gills need water to maintain their structure and prevent their thin tissues from collapsing." Also: "When out of water, the gills crumple and stick together as they dry--think of wetting some tissue paper and letting it dry." | Live Science, How do fish breathe underwater? https://www.livescience.com/how-do-fish-breathe ; UCSB Science Line https://scienceline.ucsb.edu/getkey.php?key=4461 |

| "Fresh air carries two hundred and ten millilitres of oxygen in every litre. A litre of water carries about seven." | Solid. ## Checked and NOT used
- **The bends / decompression for a gill-breather.** Fish brought up fast suffer barotrauma ("Barotrauma ('pressure shock') is the expansion of gases in a fish's swim bladder and other body cavities", NOAA Office of National Marine Sanctuaries), but that is about a swim bladder, which Dave does not have. No source says what rapid ascent would do to a person with gills, so the script stays silent (the lake in the story is shallow).
- Water "800 times denser and 50 times more viscous than air" (Macmillan): true, but the pump cost is carried by the bucket joke instead.
- NZQA student exemplar "fish use 20 % of energy to breathe": a student essay, not a source.
- Exact human lung surface area: sources range 30 to 100 m2 (Houpt lab slides say 100 m2); the script says "about seventy" (NCBI).

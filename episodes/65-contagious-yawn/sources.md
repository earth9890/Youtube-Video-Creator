# Episode 65 "Why Is Yawning Contagious?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Read in full: Provine 2005 (American Scientist reprint), Gallup & Gallup 2007 (open full text), Norscia & Palagi 2011 (PLOS ONE), Massen & Gallup 2017 (open Leiden copy). Abstracts or summaries: Guggisberg et al. 2010/2011, Campbell & de Waal 2011, Joly-Mascheroni et al. 2008, Harr et al. 2009. Dave, the quarterly figures, the man in the cardigan, the mum, the boss, the peas, the hippo and the dog scene are invented story devices; the script claims nothing from them.

| "In two thousand seven, the psychologists Andrew Gallup and Gordon Gallup proposed that yawning cools the brain. | "With a warm or room-temperature pack, forty-one percent yawned. | "According to the Gallups, contagious yawning evolved to keep groups alert. | "In two thousand eleven, Matthew Campbell and Frans de Waal showed twenty-three chimpanzees videos of yawning chimps. The chimps yawned more at familiar group members than at strangers. ## Checked and NOT used
- **"Fifty-five percent of people catch a yawn from a five-minute video"** (NPR coverage of Provine's work): not verified in a primary text. Not used. Humans overall are given as "40–60 %" in Harr et al.'s discussion; not used in the script.
- **Silva et al. 2012** (dogs yawn more at their owner's yawn, from the sound alone) and **Madsen & Persson 2012** (puppies): seen only in summaries; the dog evidence is told through the two papers I could check.
- **Gallup & Eldakar 2013 and the "thermal window"** (yawning less in hot weather, seasons): proponents' review; not used so the cooling theory is not oversold.
- **Autism and contagious yawning (Senju 2007 and later)**: the evidence depends on whether participants look at the eyes (Massen & Gallup 2017); cut for sensitivity and length.
- **Norscia, Demuru & Palagi 2016 (a gender difference in contagion)**: real, but a different sample; not used.
- **The UMBC "88 % think of yawns within thirty minutes" figure**: a news item; not verified.
- **Provine's claim that only humans and close relatives catch yawns** (his 2005 figure caption): the dog papers conflict, so it was not said.

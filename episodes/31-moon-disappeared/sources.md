# Episode 31 "What If the Moon Disappeared?": sources (verified 2026-10-20)

Dave, the switch with two settings ("on" and "gone"), the job list and "twelve hours of proper dark" are story devices, not science claims. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published measurement or result. **Debated** = the script says so out loud. **Inference** = our reasoning from a solid fact, flagged in the script or kept modest.

## Checked and NOT used
- **"If the Moon disappeared, Earth's days would become shorter"** (Science in School 2013, and a web-search summary repeating it): wrong for a Moon that vanishes today. Earth keeps its current spin; only the lengthening stops (NRAO). The 8-10 hour day in that article is for an Earth that *never had* a Moon, a different scenario.
- **Mitchell & Kirscher, *Nature Geoscience* 2023:** the day may have stalled at about 19 hours for about a billion years in the mid-Proterozoic, because solar heating of the atmosphere pushed back against the lunar brake. Real and fun, but a second chronology inside the final section; cut for length. (doi 10.1038/s41561-023-01202-6)
- **Lantink et al., *PNAS* 2022:** 2.46 billion years ago, day length 16.9 ± 0.2 h and Earth-Moon distance 321,800 km. Good data point; one coral number (Wells) does the job with less to say. (doi 10.1073/pnas.2117146119)
- **Yuan et al., *Nature* 2023:** two continent-sized blobs deep in Earth's mantle "may represent buried relics of Theia mantle material". A great "Theia is still inside us" twist, but a second twist on top of "the Moon made your night"; cut. (doi 10.1038/s41586-023-06589-1)
- **Total solar eclipses ending as the Moon recedes (~600 million years):** true in outline, but adds a seventh "job" and an uncertain date.
- **"Mass extinctions" from smaller tides** (Live Science, citing Royal Museums Greenwich): speculative and doom-shaped; the brief says no doom exaggeration. The script keeps only the narrower intertidal strip.
- **Sea turtles nesting at spring tides** (Science in School): no primary source found; not used.
- **Lions hunting better on dark nights** (Packer et al. 2011): real, but about attacks on people; off-tone for this episode.
- **Salmon & Witherington 1995, *Copeia*:** moonlight reduces how much artificial lights mislead loggerhead hatchlings. Relevant to a darker world, but the leatherback new-moon finding is cleaner.
- **Exact Tahiti range** (0.24 m in Pickard & Emery, ~70 cm in the Tetiaroa note): sources disagree, so the script gives no number.

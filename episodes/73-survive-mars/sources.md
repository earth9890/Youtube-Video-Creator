## Contested / flagged
- Whether the 600 mSv career limit "exceeds" a Mars trip depends on the assumed shielding, solar cycle and trip time: the 0.66 Sv figure is a 2013 estimate for a 180-day-each-way trip (1.8 mSv/day inside a heavily shielded cruise stage). The script says "about two thirds of a sievert" and "today's rockets".
- Real Martian soil contains perchlorate; the Wageningen crops grew in a simulant. The script says so ("a copy of the dirt, not the dirt itself").
- Vacuum survival times come from the 1950s-60s NASA/USAF animal experiments plus one human accident; the script uses a "ten to fifteen seconds" window.
- The accident year (1965 vs 1966) varies between sources.
- NASA says it has not published a separate limit for Mars crews; the single 600 mSv limit applies to all spaceflight. The script uses only that limit.

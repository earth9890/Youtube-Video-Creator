## Description
It's seven in the morning and Dave has stacked four dozen eggs on his kitchen counter. His plan: three hundred grams of protein by bedtime and enormous arms by tomorrow, because "protein is muscle". The recommended amount for Dave is about sixty-four grams. So where would all the rest go?

Dave's Brain, a small, tired librarian, tells him he's full by egg number seven. Then the day gets stranger: protein makes you burn about a quarter of its calories as heat, its nitrogen has to be turned into urea by a liver with a speed limit, and in 1928 an Arctic explorer checked into a New York hospital and ate nothing but lean meat. Then come the 100-gram shake that beat the 25-gram rule, the eight-week study that already ran Dave's experiment, and what scientists still argue about for people who eat like this every day. This is a thought experiment, not a plan.

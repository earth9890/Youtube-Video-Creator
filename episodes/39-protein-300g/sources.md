# Episode 39 "What If You Ate 300g of Protein a Day?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, his fridge scoreboard, the egg stack, the talking Brain, the old trapper and the doctor are story devices, not science claims. Dave's 80 kg body weight and his menu (12 eggs, two quarter-kilos of chicken breast, three 25 g scoops of protein powder) are invented so the numbers can be followed. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published measurement, guideline or result. **Inference** = our arithmetic or reasoning from a solid fact, kept modest in the script.

## Checked and NOT used
- **The 1954 survival-ration water estimate** (40-60 mL of extra water per gram of urea nitrogen; for Dave's ~48 g of nitrogen that is 2-3 L). True as quoted by Martin et al. 2005, but they call it "situation specific", written for desert/sea rations with limited water; cut for length and to avoid implying Dave would dehydrate.
- **Thirst / dehydration.** Hanley et al. 2001 (ACSM abstract, endurance runners at 3.6 g/kg) found "no differences were noted in daily fluid intake or thirst", though urine specific gravity and blood urea rose; Martin et al. say dehydration claims "remain speculative". So the script says "busy bathroom", never "thirsty".
- **The 1857-58 US Army party** (R. B. Marcy) that ate "five to six pounds of this meat per man daily" of starved horse and mule meat "but continued to grow weak and thin" (Speth & Spielmann 1983). True and striking; cut for length.
- **Gut absorption limit "1.3 to 10 g/h"** (Bilsborough & Mann abstract). Old estimate; Trommelen 2023's direct tracer data say more about one large meal, so not used.
- **Bilsborough & Mann's "sensible" maximum** (~25 % of energy, 2-2.5 g/kg, 176 g/d for 80 kg). Not used; kept only the absolute ceiling.
- **"Meat sweats".** No good source; used Antonio's "feeling 'hot'" and the thermic-effect numbers instead.
- **Stefansson's "approximately 50 % energy as protein" while living with the Inuit** (Bilsborough & Mann's estimate). Not used.
- **Protein and bone.** EFSA: "the available evidence does not permit the conclusion that an additional protein intake might affect bone mineral density". Off the spine.
- **Red/processed meat and cancer or heart disease.** About the food source, not protein itself; off the spine and easy to oversimplify.
- **Stefansson's likeness.** He is named in narration (historical fact) but drawn only as a generic explorer silhouette, per the no-likeness rule.

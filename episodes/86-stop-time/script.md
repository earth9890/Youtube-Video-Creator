# chapter: Nine percent
You take a step. Ow. The air in front of you is not moving. //
# hud: AIR MOLECULES: 400 M/S, BILLIONS OF BUMPS A SECOND
Normally, air molecules zip about at four hundred metres a second, and bump into each other billions of times a second. That is how they get out of your way. /
Now every one is frozen on the spot. Physicist Sean Carroll says a person stuck in that would be trapped in a prison of rigidly stationary air molecules. //
Luckily, you brought your own arm's length of moving air. You push, and it wakes the next arm's length, and the next. It is like walking through syrup. // [J]
"Brain? Is the air thick today? Or is it me?" (@dave) /
"The air is fine, Dave. It is paused. Please stop leaning on it." (@brain) // [J]
"I am not leaning. I am negotiating. It is a very firm negotiator." (@dave) // [J]

# chapter: Seven percent
After ten steps, you look up. The sky has gone out. //
# hud: LIGHT ACROSS AN ARM: 3 BILLIONTHS OF A SECOND / EYES NEED 5 TO 14 PHOTONS

You do not need much to see. In nineteen forty-two, researchers found that as few as five to fourteen photons, absorbed by the retina, will do. You are getting none. //
"Brain, why has it gone dark? It was sunny a minute ago." (@dave) /
"Light is a thing that moves, Dave. Nothing is moving. That includes the Sun." (@brain) //
"So the Sun is still up there, being sunny, and none of it is reaching me?" (@dave) /
"Correct. It is the world's biggest bulb, and you are standing in a cupboard." (@brain) // [J]
Then you remember the phone. The phone is yours, so its torch works. You get a circle of pavement the size of a dinner plate, and not an inch more. // [J]
Beyond your arm, the beam enters the paused world and simply stays there. Sean Carroll once joked that you could fix a slow world with X-rays. X-ray flashlights are hard to come by. // [J]

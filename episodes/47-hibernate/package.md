## Description
What if humans could hibernate? Could you really skip winter, or would your body quietly refuse?

Dave hangs a sign on his door that says "back in April" and goes to bed like a bear. Along the way: what a black bear actually does for seven months (its heart slows to about fourteen beats a minute), why an arctic ground squirrel can cool below freezing and Dave can't, the dwarf lemur that hibernates in a hot tree hole, the search for the brain's "switch", the space agency that wants to put astronauts to sleep on the way to Mars, and the twist that makes Dave's sign wrong: hibernators wake up every two to three weeks.

Sources:
- Tøien, Ø. et al. (2011), Science 331:906-909, "Hibernation in black bears: independence of metabolic suppression from body temperature" (ScienceDaily summary, 17 Feb 2011): bears 5-7 months without eating, drinking or toilet; metabolism about 25 % of normal; core temperature down only 5-6 degrees; heart rate about 55 to 14 beats a minute
- National Park Service, "Arctic ground squirrel": doubles its body weight in autumn; 7-8 months in hibernation; wakes every 2-3 weeks and shivers for 12-15 hours
- Barnes, B. M. (1989), Science 244:1593-1595, "Freeze avoidance in a mammal: body temperatures below 0 °C in an arctic hibernator"; Guinness World Records, "Lowest body temperature recorded in a mammal" (-2.9 °C)
- Harvard Gazette (June 2020), "Neurons that control hibernation-like behavior are discovered"; Hrvatin, S. et al. (2020), Nature 583:115-121: hypothalamus neurons that start a torpor-like state in mice; "far too soon to say" for humans
- Dausmann, K. H. et al. (2004), Nature 429:825-826, via Science News: fat-tailed dwarf lemurs, the first primates found to hibernate, in tree holes above 30 °C
- Blanco, M. B. et al. (2013), Scientific Reports, via Mongabay (2 May 2013): two more dwarf lemur species hibernating underground in cold mountain forest
- Blanco, M. B. et al. (2016), Royal Society Open Science 3:160282, "Hibernation in a primate: does sleep occur?": torpor and hibernation, scarcity, mixed results on "waking up to sleep", REM-like states above 30 °C
- Bartsiokas, A. & Arsuaga, J. L. (2020), L'Anthropologie 124(5):102797, via Archaeology magazine (22 Dec 2020): a contested argument that Spanish cave bones show hibernation-like damage; critics disagree
- European Space Agency (18 Nov 2019), "Hibernating astronauts would need smaller spacecraft"; ESA SciSpacE Topical Team on hibernation and torpor: 180-day cruise, metabolism down 75 %, spacecraft about a third lighter, 21-day recovery, roadmap of about 20 years
- Karpovich, S. A. et al. (2009), Journal of Comparative Physiology B, "Energetics of arousal episodes in hibernating arctic ground squirrels": warm-ups 86 % of the winter's energy cost at 2 °C, 23 % in a much colder burrow
- Daan, S., Barnes, B. M. & Strijkstra, A. M. (1991), Neuroscience Letters 128:265-268, "Warming up for sleep? Ground squirrels sleep during arousals from hibernation"

## Checked and NOT used
- **"Oak School" and "Spruce Elementary School."** The book calls the school "Oak School"; Discover names the real school as Spruce. The script says only "Jacobson's school."
- **Wikipedia's claim that Rosenthal "originally claimed … 24.8 IQ points in excess of the gain shown by the controls" and that only 5-10 % of students show effects.** Not found in the book's Table 7-1 (which shows +15.4 in grade one, +3.8 overall) and attributed to secondary sources; not used.
- **The Discover article's claim that no one has replicated the effects "without first telling lies."** Contested by the Auckland experiment (which trained teachers rather than lying) and by the author's own later paragraphs; framed in the script as "not labels but habits".
- **That Rosenthal's design was expectancy raised "and lowered".** The book says only raising was used for ethical reasons; the "Pygmalion/Golem" two-way version applies to the rats ("maze-dull") only.
- **Wikipedia's "Galatea effect" (Eden & Ravid) and Eden's claim that telling trainees directly raises performance.** Secondary only; cut.
- **The OKCupid couples anecdote and the JAMA neighborhoods study (both in Discover).** Interesting but tangential, not independently verified.
- **Albert Shanker's 1968 column criticizing the study.** Political context in Discover; cut for length.
- **Dov Eden's "great science that is underapplied."** A good quote but cut for length.
- **Exact experimental N for first and second grade separately (7 and 12).** Inferred from a garbled table scan; the script uses only the verified total of nineteen (Table 7-2).

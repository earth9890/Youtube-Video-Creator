# Episode 40 "What If You Drank 100 Energy Drinks?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, the hundred cans, "paper Dave", the library chairs, the corridor queue and the talking Brain are story devices, not science claims. Everything the narrator or the doctor states as fact is below. **Solid** = published figure or finding. **Inference** = our arithmetic or plain-language reading of a solid fact, kept modest in the script.

**The "can" in this episode is a typical small 250 ml can with about 80 mg of caffeine and up to about 28 g of sugar** (BfR, healthdirect, Health Canada, NZ HealthEd below). Bigger cans exist (the FDA gives 54–328 mg per 16 fl oz); the script always says "a typical small can", and the hundred-can totals use the small can.

## Checked and NOT used
- **Harmful or lethal dose figures, left out on purpose (safety brief: no doses-to-harm):** FDA's "toxic effects, like seizures, can be observed with rapid consumption of around 1,200 milligrams of caffeine"; IOM 2001's estimated fatal oral dose ("10–14 g") and "extreme side effects... at caffeine intakes of 1 g"; Cleveland Clinic's "symptoms can develop after drinking about a gallon (3 to 4 liters) of water over an hour or two". The script says only that large amounts can be dangerous, cites the hospital figures, and never puts a number on harm. The 8,000 mg hundred-can total is said as "twenty times the day's guidance", not in milligrams.
- **Bigger cans:** FDA "54 to 328 milligrams of caffeine per 16 fluid ounces"; Harvard's "typical" US drink "around 40 grams of sugar... 160 mg or more of caffeine"; Antonio et al. 2024 (top 75 US drinks average 174 mg per serving). True, but the script sticks to one can size so the counting stays honest; the note at the top of this file covers it.
- **Health Canada's "2 servings of a typical energy drink per day"** (2010–2011 assessment) versus its later "5 energy drinks" ≈ 400 mg: two different framings from the same agency; we used EFSA and FDA instead.
- **"Caffeine crash" / adenosine rebound:** popular explanation (Reddit ELI5, fact sites), but Reichert 2022 says the next-morning effect "remains to be tested". Script only says the adenosine "waits" and is "still there".
- **Medmate's "half-life around 5.5 hours" and "ten hours to clear"**: pharmacy blog, secondary; IOM 2001 used instead.
- **Energy-drink-related deaths and named lawsuits**: real reports exist, but they involve specific brands and real people; not needed and against the brief.
- **Stomach capacity** ("holds about a litre comfortably"): not sourced well enough; not used.
- **Taurine, guarana, B vitamins**: EFSA says other constituents at usual levels "would not affect the safety of single doses of caffeine up to 200mg"; off-topic for the story.

# chapter: 3:56 pm
You step into the garden. And everything is a bright white blur. //
# hud: AIR 1.0003 / CORNEA 1.376 / CORNEA = TWO-THIRDS OF FOCUSING
In eighteen ninety-seven, H. G. Wells explained how to vanish. A see-through thing disappears when light passes through it as if it were not there, because its refractive index matches the air. /
Your eye works the opposite way. Your cornea bends light, because it is denser than air, and does about two thirds of the focusing. Painted see-through, it bends nothing. //
Open your eyes underwater and you get the same blur, because water has about the same index as your cornea. You are wearing a swimming pool on each eye. // [J]
"Brain, I can see light, but I cannot see anything in it. What is going on?" (@dave) /
"Your lenses do not bend any more, Dave. Everything arrives everywhere at once." (@brain) /
"So it's like a very bright, very expensive fog?" (@dave) /
"It is like being inside a lamp. Please stop walking into the lamp." (@brain) // [J]

# chapter: 4:04 pm
You rub your eyes. It does not help. There is a second problem. //
# hud: RETINA: STOPS ABOUT 1 IN 5 PHOTONS
To see, your retina must stop the light. Science writer Philip Ball says Wells realised that would make a person blind, because the retina has to absorb light to work at all. /
In nineteen forty-two, Hecht and his colleagues found that at least eighty percent of the light already passes through a normal retina. Yours is now at a hundred. //
"I painted my eyes see-through. So the light goes straight out the back of my head?" (@dave) /
"Yes. Congratulations. You are a window with opinions." (@brain) // [J]
In the book, a trace of pigment stays behind the Invisible Man's retinas, fainter than mist. So you ask the paint to leave the back of your eyes. It is story paint. It agrees. //
You blink. The garden swims into view, wobbly but there. You can see again. //
# gag: the paint drains off the backs of two round eyes like a slow blind going up. Not gory: just a squeak.

# Episode 87 "What If You Turned Invisible?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, Mr Pike, Leo, Doctor Hale, the Brain and the jumble-sale paint are story devices. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published statement or measurement. **Inference** = our reasoning from a solid fact. **Story** = a device, never stated as science. Quoted strings are exact as returned by the page or the book; unquoted figures are as the page states them.

| Script line | Claim | Exact quote / number | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "In eighteen ninety-seven, H. G. Wells explained how to vanish. A see-through thing disappears when light passes through it as if it were not there, because its refractive index matches the air." | Solid. | Griffin: "a transparent thing becomes invisible if it is put in any medium of almost the same refractive index. And if you will consider only a second, you will see also that the powder of glass might be made to vanish in air, if its refractive index could be made the same as that of air; for then there would be no refraction or reflection as the light passed from glass to air." | H. G. Wells, The Invisible Man (1897), ch. 19, Project Gutenberg: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5230/pg5230.txt |
| "Your cornea bends light, because it is denser than air, and does about two thirds of the focusing." | Solid (the cornea with the anterior chamber and lens gives two-thirds of the eye's optical power; the cornea itself is the main bending surface). | Wikipedia, Cornea: the cornea "has a refractive index of 1.376" and, together with the anterior chamber and lens, accounts for "approximately two-thirds of the eye's total optical power". Air at STP is 1.000273 and water at 20 C is 1.333. | Wikipedia, Cornea: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornea ; Wikipedia, List of refractive indices: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_refractive_indices |
| "Painted see-through, it bends nothing." | **Inference** (a perfectly see-through cornea with n = 1 has no index step, so no refraction). | - | Follows from Wells (above) |
| "Open your eyes underwater and you get the same blur, because water has about the same index as your cornea." | Solid. | "Water, however, has approximately the same refractive index as the cornea (both about 1.33)", "effectively eliminating the cornea's focusing properties". Underwater the light is "focused behind the retina, resulting in an extremely blurred image". | Wikipedia, Underwater vision: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwater_vision |
| "Science writer Philip Ball says Wells realised that would make a person blind, because the retina has to absorb light to work at all." | Solid. | "Wells realised that this would make a person blind, because the retina has to absorb light to work at all - but he felt he could ignore that." | Philip Ball, "Vanishing point: five ways to become invisible", The Guardian, 19 July 2014: https://www.theguardian.com/science/2014/jul/19/vanishing-point-five-ways-to-become-invisible |
| "In nineteen forty-two, Hecht and his colleagues found that at least eighty percent of the light already passes through a normal retina." | Solid. | "...of the rest, at least 80 per cent passes through the retina without being absorbed." | "So they hover in the air, two little black dots, about six centimetres apart." / "Your retinas have to stay dark to catch the light." | **Inference** (our reading of Wells and Ball: whatever absorbs light must remain visible; the spacing is an ordinary eye separation, about 6 cm). | - | Ball (above); Wells (above) |
| "A scientist at Duke said no perfect cloak would let you see out." / "A cloak bends light around you, so no light reaches your eyes." | Solid. | Stephane Larouche, Duke Meta Group: "Because of this reciprocity principle, no perfect invisibility cloak would allow you to see outside." Also Greg Gbur (Scientific American, 2011): "an outside observer won't see a cloaked person, but the cloaked person won't see anything!" | Atlas Obscura, "The Problem With Invisibility Is The Blindness" (Eric Grundhauser, 11 Mar 2016): https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/the-problem-with-invisibility-is-the-blindness ; Scientific American guest blog (Gbur, 11 Jan 2011): https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/guest-blog/invisibility-after-several-years-of-research-its-just-gotten-weirder/ |

## Caveats on the evidence (kept off screen, stated here)
- **End of history illusion is contested.** The 2013 design is cross-sectional and the "past change" side relies on memory. Christian Jarrett (BPS Research Digest, 14 Jan 2013): "It would be easier to believe in the End of History Illusion if there was data on actual change"; he also notes the band study's two groups knew different things. The authors compared their reports with real ten-year change in 3,808 MIDUS adults and found reported change "almost identical" to actual change and both "substantially larger" than predicted change (different scales, so via intraclass correlations). Harris & Busseri (2019, *Journal of Research in Personality* 83) found no illusion for *life satisfaction* in three waves of MIDUS data; Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson replied (2020, *JRP* 86) that the same data show it at the individual level; Busseri & Harris replied again (2020, *JRP* 88). The script therefore says only what people *answered* ("A lot." / "Not much."), not "you will change X %". Independent support that personality really does change most in early adulthood: Roberts, Walton & Viechtbauer 2006 (*Psychological Bulletin* 132:1-25), meta-analysis of 92 longitudinal samples: increases in social dominance, conscientiousness and emotional stability, "especially in young adulthood (age 20 to 40)". Sources: https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/end-history-illusion-illusion · https://midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/1997.pdf · https://midus.wisc.edu/findings/pdfs/2073.pdf · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16435954/
- **Rogers & Feller is one MOOC.** Assignment to essays was random, so the effect is causal for that course; the 45 % is the top 100 portfolios, a small group. Study 2's mechanism data are from 361 online participants.
- **Azoulay et al. is US data, 2007-2014, firms with at least one employee.** The middle-aged edge is partly because more middle-aged people start firms, which is why the script also gives the per-founder rate (1.8x).

## Checked and NOT used
- **NAR 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers: median first-time buyer age 40, a record** (press release 4 Nov 2025: "the typical age of first-time buyers climbed to an all-time high of 40 years"; transactions July 2024-June 2025). Would answer Dave's "house" item, but the figure is disputed: Redfin puts the median "somewhere in the mid-30s", AEI's Edward Pinto says it hasn't risen much (Marketplace, 17 Apr 2026), and I could not open NAR's own page for the often-quoted "median age 29 in 1981" comparison (only secondary retellings). It is also US-only and mostly about affordability, which would make the episode sadder. https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom/first-time-home-buyer-share-falls-to-historic-low-of-21-median-age-rises-to-40 · https://www.marketplace.org/story/2026/04/17/firsttime-homebuyers-keep-getting-older
- **Jordan, Monin, Dweck et al. 2011 ("Misery has more company than people think", *PSPB* 37:120-135):** students underestimated how many peers had a bad two weeks (low grade: guessed 44.1 %, real 60.0 %) and overestimated fun (party: guessed 62.2 %, real 41.3 %); 40 % of negative emotions were "effortfully hidden" vs 13 % of positive ones. Verified (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4138214), but it is the "others' lives look better" idea that episode 21 already covered with Deri et al. 2017, so it was cut to avoid repeating ep21.
- **Steve Jobs launched the iPhone at 52** (Azoulay et al.: "Steve Jobs and Apple computer appeared to find their blockbuster innovation with the iPhone, introduced when Jobs was 52"). True and fun, but a famous-person example is the survivorship move episodes 20 and 24 warned against; the per-founder rate does the job.
- **Zuckerberg's "I only own a mattress"** (same 2007 talk, Infopackets): verified, cut for length.
- **"Over 30, people are a little slow"**: a paraphrase that later coverage attributes to Zuckerberg; I found no contemporary verbatim source, so not used.
- **US median age at first marriage (Census, about 30 for men, 28-29 for women in 2024):** would answer a "married by thirty" item; not in Dave's list, and a third median would make it a statistics list.
- **Quoidbach band numbers ($129 to see today's favourite band in ten years vs $80 for a once-favourite band now; "61% more"):** verified in the paper (Study 4, 170 MTurk adults), but the sample is small and Jarrett's critique applies, so the script uses Thunder Wolf as a story illustration without the dollar figures.
- **Episode overlap:** episode 06 (old motivational format) said, without a source, that "the people furthest ahead of you don't inspire you. They crush you." Rogers & Feller is the named, numbered evidence for that idea; no ep06 line or fact is reused. None of the facts from episodes 13-26 are used (checked against their scripts and sources, including Sanders, Hartshorne & Germine, the juggling study, Maguire's taxi drivers, Dyson, the testing effect, Deri, Gilovich, Boothby and Wang).

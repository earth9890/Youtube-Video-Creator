# Episode 14 "You Are Not Ugly": sources (verified 2026-10-13)

| Script line | Claim | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "at one foot, a nose looks thirty percent wider than at five feet" | A selfie taken about 12 in from the face makes the nasal base look about 30 % wider than a photo from 5 ft (standard portrait distance). Authors: Brittany Ward, Max Ward, Ohad Fried, Boris Paskhover (Rutgers + Stanford). Paskhover: a selfie is "in essence looking into a portable funhouse mirror". | Ward B. et al., "Nasal Distortion in Short-Distance Photographs: The Selfie Effect", *JAMA Facial Plastic Surgery*, published online 1 March 2018 (doi 10.1001/jamafacial.2018.0009). Press release: https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/03/180301115349.htm · Abstract: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323497618_Nasal_Distortion_in_Short-Distance_Photographs_The_Selfie_Effect |
| "women preferred their mirror image; their friends, the real one" | Each woman reliably preferred the mirror print of her own face, and her close friend or lover tended to prefer the true print (mere-exposure effect). Study 1: 37 women + friends. Study 2: 38 women; 20 of 28 preferred the mirror print, 19 of 28 lovers preferred the true print. Small samples, so the script makes no percentage claim. | Mita T.H., Dermer M., Knight J., "Reversed Facial Images and the Mere-Exposure Hypothesis", *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 1977;35(8):597-601. PDF: https://sites.uwm.edu/dermer/files/2016/07/mita_reversed-1a0ku8p.pdf |
| "one person's photos vary in looks more than different people do"; "one photo of Dave can look less like Dave than a stranger" | Abstract: "within-person variability exceeded between-person variability in attractiveness". Photos of the same person often look more different than photos of different people (identity judgements). | Jenkins R., White D., Van Montfort X., Burton A.M., "Variability in photos of the same face", *Cognition* 2011;121(3):313-323. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010027711002022 · https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/variability-in-photos-of-the-same-face/ |
| "Caltech found faces shot further away look more attractive" | Faces photographed from inside personal space (45 cm) got lower ratings of trustworthiness, competence and attractiveness than the same faces at 135 cm, and lower investments in a trust game. Caltech. | Bryan R., Perona P., Adolphs R., "Perspective Distortion from Interpersonal Distance Is an Implicit Visual Cue for Social Judgments of Faces", *PLoS ONE* 2012;7(9):e45301. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0045301 · Caltech press: https://neurosciencenews.com/ready-for-your-close-up-perception-neuroscience/ |

## Checked and NOT used
- **"People rate themselves less attractive than strangers do."** Not supported as a general claim. Greitemeyer (2020, *Scand. J. Psychol.*, 6 studies) found most people rate themselves *more* attractive than strangers do; only the most attractive underrate themselves. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/sjop.12631
- **Profile-photo choice (White, Sutherland & Burton 2017, *Cogn. Res. Princ. Implic.*, n = 610):** strangers chose photos of you that made better impressions than the ones you chose. The effect was strongest for trustworthiness and competence, weaker for attractiveness, so the script doesn't use it. Backup if needed: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5391387/
- **Spotlight effect (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000):** Barry Manilow T-shirt wearers guessed that about half the room noticed; the real figure was about 23 %. Verified, but cut for length. A good fact for a future "everyone is staring at me" episode. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotlight_effect
- **Liking gap (Boothby et al. 2018):** about conversations, not looks. Not used.

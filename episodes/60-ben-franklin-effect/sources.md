# Episode 60 "The Ben Franklin Effect": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Read in full text: Franklin's *Autobiography* (Project Gutenberg edition, the clerk passage and the 1731 library passage, plus the editor's introduction). Read in abstract: Niiya 2016 (PubMed), Flynn & Lake 2008 (PubMed). Used as secondary summaries: Wikipedia ("Benjamin Franklin effect", "Cognitive dissonance") for Jecker & Landy 1969, Festinger & Carlsmith 1959, the 1971 kind/harsh experiment and the converse effect; the Jecker & Landy paper itself is paywalled (its SAGE page lists only references), so no numbers from it are quoted. Dave, Gerald, the muffins, the leaf blower, the car, the book "Privet and Its Discontents" and the ladder are invented story devices.

## Checked and NOT used
- **Numbers from Jecker & Landy 1969** (sample sizes, rating means, effect sizes). One blog gives 74 participants; the paper was paywalled and the figure could not be verified. Not used.
- **A claim that the Franklin effect has been "replicated many times" or "failed to replicate".** Practice-site pages assert both without citing studies. The script says only that we could not find a large, modern, preregistered replication.
- **Wikipedia's note that Niiya's study "reproduced" the effect "in a smaller study."** True as a description; the script calls it modest and notes that helping without being asked showed no increase.
- **Schopler & Compere 1971 details.** Only the one-line summary appears in Wikipedia/McRaney; the original was not opened; the script uses it in a single hedged sentence.
- **Dale Carnegie's attribution of the flattery idea.** Not verified in Carnegie's own text; the script says "a third reading" without naming him.
- **Soldiers, grudges and Hatfields vs McCoys** (Wikipedia "Converse" section). Dark, thinly sourced; only the one-sentence converse effect is used.
- **Mentor/sales-training uses of the effect.** Practitioner claims.
- **Whether the man in Franklin's story can be identified.** Franklin names neither the man nor the book; historians' guesses were not checked and not used.
- **Franklin's age at 1737.** Stated as thirty at 1736 (born 17 January 1706).

How read: "(read)" = I read the page text or the open-access paper myself; "(fetch)" = a fetch tool returned the quote or a summary of the page, so confirm the wording against the page before quoting on screen. Primary sources for 1518 itself are chronicles and city archives; none were read directly (they are in German, French and Latin). The script relies on historians' accounts of them and flags every contested point.

## Checked and NOT used
- **"Saba" shrine / "Saverne" naming:** the shrine is usually given as Saverne (Pennant-Rea, Liddell); Rue89 calls it Saint-Guy du Holenstein near Saverne. Script says "near Saverne".
- **Smallpox and syphilis as the specific stressors:** appears in a search summary of Waller, but I could not confirm it in a page I read (the Lancet page was blocked). The script uses only the "starvation and disease" wording that Wikipedia quotes.
- **Bartholomew's heretical-dancing-sect theory:** mentioned by Waller as a suggestion that contemporaries did not treat as heresy; not needed.
- **Paracelsus's view that Troffea danced to embarrass her husband:** from Pennant-Rea alone; not used.
- **"Over one hundred died" and "fifteen a day for a brief period at least":** popular retellings; no primary found. Not stated as fact.
- **The Imlin chronicle date and the poem in the archives ("many died"):** Liddell's page reports an archive poem saying "many died"; this conflicts with Rue89 ("no deaths in the archives") and I could not trace it, so it is not used. Flagged here as an open conflict.
- **Waller's 1963 laughing-epidemic date (Pennant-Rea):** Wikipedia gives 30 January 1962; the script uses 1962 and the school figures from Wikipedia.
- **Primary texts:** the Strasbourg council minutes (R3, fol. 72 recto per Pennant-Rea), Brant's annals, Paracelsus, Waller's book (2009) and the Lancet article were not read directly.

## Unsure facts (for the owner)
1. Whether the city's papers truly record no deaths rests on one exhibition report (Rue89) quoting Clementz; the chronicle tradition says deaths. The script presents both and says "may have".
2. "The remaining dancers had been sent to the shrine" in September: the sources are popular (NatGeo, Wikipedia).
3. Galen's dates and "bestseller" are general knowledge.

## Checked and NOT used
- **Herpes and the kiss (Arbøll & Rasmussen 2023):** Mesopotamian medical texts describe *bu'shanu*, a mouth disease "reminiscent of" HSV-1, and the authors argue the kiss was not a sudden trigger for herpes spread because kissing was old and widespread. Skipped on the brief's advice: medical, disputed, and not family-friendly in a stick-figure date story.
- **Bonobo "tongue" kisses and the San Diego zookeeper anecdote (de Waal 1995):** true and funny, but adult; the script only says kisses "can mean many things".
- **Mesopotamian love incantations, the priestess kiss that "causes" loss of speech, and the skull-kissing cure for teeth grinding (Arbøll 2025):** real, but adult, morbid or too long.
- **Kunik (Inuit):** often called the "Eskimo kiss", but it is a nose-and-upper-lip sniff used mostly with family and children, and Jankowiak found romantic kissing in nine of 11 circum-Arctic communities. Using it as "instead of kissing" would be wrong, so we used the Māori hongi as a greeting example instead.
- **Darwin's list of cultures "without" kissing (New Zealanders, Tahitians, Papuans, Australians, Somals, "Esquimaux"):** 19th-century travellers' reports with outdated labels; Arctic peoples did kiss per Jankowiak. Quoted only his general sentence.
- **Mehinaku of Brazil found kissing "gross":** press reports via the BBC; the authors' own essay says they reacted with disgust but adds an adult detail. Thonga and Trobriand quotes are better sourced.
- **Head-turn follow-ups:** Sedgewick, Holtslander & Elias 2019 (*Journal of Nonverbal Behavior*) found no right bias in first kisses between strangers (51 % vs 49 %, 230 couples); parent-child kisses lean left. The script only claims Güntürkün's couples ("two in three kissing couples"), so no correction needed, but worth a pinned comment if viewers ask.
- **Floyd et al. 2009 (kissing more lowered cholesterol in 52 adults):** health claim; the series avoids those.
- **Hendrie & Brewer 2010 (kissing protects against cytomegalovirus in pregnancy):** speculative medical hypothesis.
- **Weyrich 2017 shared mouth microbe as proof humans and Neanderthals kissed:** saliva could also be shared through food; used only as background for Brindle's "probably".
- **French cheek-kiss direction by city (Chapelain 2016):** greeting kisses, off topic.

# Episode 89 "What If You Found a Tiny Black Hole in Your Pocket?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, the jumble-sale coat, the biscuit tin, the clerk, the child, Doctor Hale and the Brain are story devices. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published statement or standard formula. **Inference** = our arithmetic from published formulas (inputs given, so anyone can redo them; computed 2026-10-22 in Python). **Hypothetical** = primordial black holes have never been observed; the script says "if" and "could". **Story** = a device, never stated as science. Quoted strings are exact as returned by the page; unquoted figures are as the page states them.

Working mass: 10^12 kg (a billion tonnes), the lowest mass that would survive to today (Khriplovich et al.; Wikipedia on Primordial black holes: "originally having masses lower than 10^12 kg would not have survived to the present").

## Checked and NOT used
- The idea that a billion-tonne hole is "the size of an atom": it is smaller. The brief's "size of an atom" corresponds to the heavier, asteroid-mass holes (Wikipedia: "the mass of an asteroid but the size of a hydrogen atom"); the script uses the billion-tonne one, which is smaller than an atom and about a proton in size.
- LHC safety claims (Wikipedia, Micro black hole: such black holes "would instantly evaporate", cosmic rays "do not produce any damage"): not needed for the story; left out.
- The detail that the hole would grow by accreting the Earth: no source found for a timescale at this mass; the script does not claim it either way, only that the glow is a tiny fraction of the Earth's own heat.
- Britannica's phrasing of Hawking's "billion tons in the space of a proton": not retrievable (HTTP 403), so the script uses Hamilton, Khriplovich et al. and Wikipedia instead.
- Any "explodes with the energy of a million Hiroshimas" figure for a mini black hole: unsourced and alarming; replaced with Hamilton's own sentence.

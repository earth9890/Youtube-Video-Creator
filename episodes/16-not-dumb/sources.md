## Checked and NOT used
- **Juggling (Draganski et al. 2004, *Nature* 427:311-312):** 24 young adults learned a three-ball cascade over 3 months, and grey matter in the mid-temporal area (hMT/V5) increased. It shrank partly after 3 months of no practice but stayed above baseline. Verified, but cut for length because the taxi study makes the same point with stronger story beats. https://www.nature.com/articles/427311a
- **Maguire et al. 2000 (PNAS), cross-sectional taxi study:** superseded by the 2011 before-and-after study, which shows the change happens rather than only a correlation.
- **Taxi trade-off:** qualifiers did *worse* than controls at recalling a complex figure after 30 min, and the sample was all male. Not mentioned; the script only says the map room grew.
- **Taxi caveat:** practice hours were self-chosen, so motivation could also differ between groups. The script says "their secret: twice the weekly practice", which is the reported difference, not a proven sole cause.
- **Roediger simplification:** "three times" is the average 3.4 reads. "Remembered forty percent" means idea units recalled in free recall of a prose passage. The result extends only to one week.
- **"We only use 10 % of our brain":** a myth, but it answers no question this story asks.
- **Ebbinghaus forgetting curve / spacing effect:** real and well replicated (Dunlosky 2013 rates distributed practice "high utility"). Left out to keep one action (self-testing) and stay under 190 words.
- **Growth mindset:** deliberately not mentioned. Large replications show small or null effects (Sisk et al. 2018, *Psychological Science*: two meta-analyses, mindset–achievement r ≈ 0.10, intervention d ≈ 0.08: https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2018/05/study-finds-popular-growth-mindset-educational-interventions-arent-very-effective), so the script makes no mindset claim, only the brain-change and testing evidence.
- **"Students prefer rereading" (Karpicke 2009 survey):** challenged by Kuhbandner & Emmerdinger 2019, so the script does not say "most students re-read". It says only that re-reading *feels* like knowing (backed by Roediger & Karpicke 2006's confidence ratings).

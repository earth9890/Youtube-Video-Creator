## Checked and NOT used
- **Whillans, Christie, Cheung, Jordan & Chen (2017), *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*:** UBC first-years thought peers had more close friends (own mean 3.6 vs estimate 4.2; 48 % thought others had more, 31 % fewer) and blamed visibility: social activities happen in public, solitary ones don't. Strong support for the "quiet nights don't get posted" idea, but cut to keep one named study per beat. https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/psychology/think-other-people-have-more-friends-you-youre-probably-wrong
- **US Surgeon General's 2023 advisory ("about one in two adults")** and Gallup/Meta 2023 (24 % "very or fairly lonely"): different questions and samples. WHO 2025 is the most recent worldwide figure, so the script uses only it.
- **"Loneliness is as bad as fifteen cigarettes a day"** and WHO's "871,000 deaths a year": health claims the series promise forbids beyond the sources and that don't answer the story's question. Not used.
- **ATUS socialising trend (35 min a day in 2025 vs 41 in 2015):** true, but it would make the video sadder and doesn't answer "where are the ninety-one?".
- **Deri debiasing study:** telling people to compare with less social people raised life satisfaction. True, but the script's action is one message, to keep one action.
- **Liu caveat:** the studies covered people with a positive past relationship; the script says only "people are glad to hear from us", not "anyone, after any falling-out".

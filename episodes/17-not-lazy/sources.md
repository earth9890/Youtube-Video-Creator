## Checked and NOT used
- **Placebo pill:** the original mood-freezing procedure (Manucia, Baumann & Cialdini 1984) used placebo pills. Tice et al. used a candle (Studies 2 and 3) and instructions (Study 1). Some blogs say Tice used a pill; the paper says candle. The script says candle.
- **Boring distractors:** upset students with "changeable" moods and only boring distractors (a preschool puzzle, out-of-date technical journals) wasted 8.18 min, about the same as everyone else. True and supportive, but cut for length.
- **Replication caveat:** no direct replication of Study 3 was found. It is one lab study of 88 students (about 11 per cell). The script reports it as one experiment and does not generalise the numbers.
- **"Two-minute rule":** David Allen's original rule (Getting Things Done) is "if it takes less than two minutes, do it now", which is a different idea. We don't cite it.
- **Tice & Baumeister 1997 (Psychological Science):** procrastinating students had less stress early in term but more stress and illness and lower grades later. Real, but it answers no question this story asks.
- **"Up to one in five adults are chronic procrastinators"** (Ferrari): used in episode 1; not needed here.
- **Self-forgiveness study (Wohl, Pychyl & Bennett 2010):** used in episode 1; not repeated.
- **DayOne Films, "You're Not Lazy. You're Too Comfortable":** topic inspiration only. Our story (Dave, the oven, the candle experiment) and every line are original.

# Episode 82 "What If You Could See in the Dark?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, Mina, Mr Biscuit, the cat guide, the farmer, the cyclist, the head-torch man and "your legs are still yours" are story devices, not science claims. Everything the narrator or the cast states as fact is below. **Solid** = published source. **Inference** = our reasoning from a solid fact.

| Script line | Claim | Exact quote / number | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Rods. About a hundred and twenty million of them." | Solid (textbook figure; counts vary by source, e.g. 92 million total photoreceptors in one 2015 review). | "The rods are more numerous, some 120 million... The 6 to 7 million cones provide the eye's color sensitivity". Lamb 2016: "4.6 million out [of] 92 million" are cones. | HyperPhysics, The Rods and Cones of the Human Eye: http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/vision/rodcone.html ; Lamb, "Why rods and cones?", Eye 2016: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763127/ |
| "Each one can notice a single particle of light." | Solid. | "rods are so sensitive that they can detect a single photon of light"; Scientific American: "rods can respond reliably to a single visible light photon, so they operate at the physical limit of light detection." | Kefalov, "Rod and Cone Visual Pigments and Phototransduction...", J Biol Chem 2011: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3265844/ ; Scientific American: https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/experts-eyes-adjust-to-darkness/ |
| "Your six million cones do the colour. In the dark, they clock off." | Solid. | Kefalov: cones "cannot signal in dim light conditions, depriving us of color vision at night." | Kefalov (above) |

| "Tapetum lucidum. A mirror behind the retina. Also NIDEK: a cat's eyesight is "approximately one-tenth that of a human". | The Conversation (above); NIDEK: https://www.nidek-intl.com/eyestories/vol-10-why-do-cats-eyes-light-up/ |

## Checked and NOT used
- "Cats see 6x better because of the tapetum alone": Kang et al. 2009 found the cat's advantage is mostly optics (pupil, focal length), the tapetum adding about 29 % more light. The script says only "about six times better" and describes the tapetum separately.
- Owl eye image "2.7 times brighter than a human's" (BTO, tawny owl): true but one fact too many; left out.
- Rod/cone totals (120 million vs 92 million): reported both ways; the script uses the HyperPhysics textbook numbers and the sources row flags it.

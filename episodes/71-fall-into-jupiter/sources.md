# Episode 71 "What If You Fell Into Jupiter?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, his flag, the gauge, the Brain and the suit made of imagination are story devices. The trip follows NASA's Galileo atmospheric probe (7 December 1995) for the first hour, then published models and Juno results. **Solid** = published measurement. **Inference** = our arithmetic, kept modest in the script. **Contested/open** = labelled in the script. ## Contested / flagged
- Wind speed: papers give ~170 m/s (about 610 km/h) below 4 bar; NASA's trivia gives a different figure with unclear units; the script uses the papers. A later review notes the wind variation between 0.4 and 4 bar might partly reflect hot-spot dynamics.
- 228 g (NASA profile) versus ~350 g (NASA trivia): the script uses 228.
- Depth of the metallic transition: published values vary, so the script says "thousands of kilometres down, and they disagree by a lot".
- The NASA entry timeline contains pre-planned entries (the vaporisation estimate); "room temperature at 5:34" is also from that timeline and is consistent with Seiff's adiabat (our check above).
- Juno's equatorial water figure may not apply to the whole planet; stated in the script.

## Checked and NOT used
- Probe entry temperature at the shield "hotter than the Sun's surface": no source read; compare not used.
- "A human would stop sinking where density matches": used only as Astronomy.com's statement about a dropped probe, hedged with "probably".
- Probe descent figure "112 miles (180 km) below its entry ceiling" (NASA) and "80 to 90 miles" (Discover 1996): different reference levels; we use Seiff's 133 km below the 1-bar level.
- Peak entry temperature 16,000 C is a single NASA value; kept but unrepeated.
- Metallic hydrogen core pressure and temperature numbers (e.g. 70 Mbar, 20,000 K) differ across sources: not used.

# Episode 67 "The Patients Who Woke Up After 40 Years": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

How read: "(read)" = I read the page text myself; "(fetch)" = a fetch tool returned the quote or a summary, so confirm the wording against the page before showing it on screen. The patient material comes from Oliver Sacks's own accounts (book, 1985 radio interview). A December 2025 New Yorker investigation says Sacks embellished some details, so every patient detail in the script is attributed ("Sacks said", "Sacks recalled") and the twist chapter says so. Only anonymised book names are used (Rose R., Leonard L.); no real patient identity appears.

## Checked and NOT used
- **Real patient identities (e.g. "Lowe"), the film's actors, Pinter's play "A Kind of Alaska":** the brief says anonymised names only; the film and play add nothing the story needs.
- **"About eighty patients" and "about twenty treated":** one search summary gave each; neither was confirmed in a source I read, so the script says "a group of his own patients".
- **1966 start at Beth Abraham:** from a search summary of Wikipedia; not needed.
- **Rose R. "admitted in 1935":** an informal source; not used.
- **"Most of the original twenty ... lost their battle; only a handful alive by the 1990s":** ScienceInsights (unclear sourcing); not used.
- **Wikipedia's range of death toll (500,000 vs 1.6 million):** sources conflict; script says "more than a million affected, a third of them died in the acute phase" per Hoffman and Vilensky.
- **Pandemic end year (1926 vs 1927 vs 1930s):** conflicting; script only says "continuing into the 1930s" via peaks.
- **Aviv's article itself, and Weschler's quote about patients' conditions:** the article was blocked; I did not repeat unverified second-hand claims about conditions at the hospital.
- **Sacks's private claim about how many details he embellished:** not quantified in anything I could read; the script says "some details".

## Unsure facts (for the owner)
1. All patient scenes rest on Sacks's own telling (the script flags this and the twist says it directly). If the owner wants the twist softer or harder, the evidence is Aviv's piece, which I could only read secondhand.
2. The Duvoisin abstract's numbers (30 treated, 7 withdrawn, 26 assessed) are inconsistent; the script quotes 30 and 26 as printed.
3. Cotzias 1967 used DL-dopa; "L-DOPA" in the script is the later, active form (flagged in the line).
4. The "Moon" line is general history, not a claim about the patients.

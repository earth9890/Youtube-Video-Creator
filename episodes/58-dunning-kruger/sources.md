## Checked and NOT used
- **That McArthur Wheeler "inspired" the study.** The paper only says they "bring up the unfortunate affairs of Mr. Wheeler to make three points"; the script says they "opened their paper with him".
- **"The less intelligent are more confident than the smart."** The paper and its later summaries stress that bottom-quartile participants still rated themselves below top-quartile participants in the logic and grammar studies; the script says so.
- **Where "Mount Stupid" came from.** Blogs attribute it to a 2011 webcomic or to mid-2000s management content; none is reliable. The script says only that it is not in the paper and its origin is unclear.
- **Claims that Dunning himself disowned the graph.** Single-source, no primary quote found. Not used.
- **Wikipedia's "the first rule of the Dunning-Kruger club" quote.** Secondary; not used.
- **The "mostly a statistical artefact" claim as settled.** Other work (Jansen et al. 2021; Dunkel et al. 2022; a later British birth-cohort study per an IZA working paper) finds an effect. The script presents the dispute as unsettled. Hiller (2023) and Gignac & Zajenkowski's reply were seen only in abstract/summary form and not used.
- **The paper's anosognosia analogy and "Thomas Gray was right: ignorance is bliss."** Sensitive/neurological or off-spine; not used.
- **The paper's Darwin (1871) quote, "ignorance more frequently begets confidence than does knowledge."** Verified in the text but cut for length.
- **Richard Nisbett on Amos Tversky ("The quicker you realize that Amos is smarter than you, the smarter you yourself must be").** Personal communication; cut.
- **Exact sample counts for the grammar and logic bottom quartiles in the script.** Only Study 1 (n = 65) and Study 4 (n = 140) are named.

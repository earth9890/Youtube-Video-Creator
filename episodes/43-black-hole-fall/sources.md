# Episode 43 "What If You Fell Into a Black Hole?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, his postcard, his torch, the suit readout, the ship and the news anchor are story devices, not science claims. Dave's trip follows NASA's 2024 simulated plunge into a black hole of 4.3 million Suns (Sgr A*'s mass); the gas around the real Sgr A* is left out, as a thought experiment. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published measurement, result or textbook statement. **Inference** = our arithmetic or simplification from a solid fact, kept modest in the script. **Contested** = labelled as argued about in the script.

## Checked and NOT used
- "At the photon sphere you could see the back of your own head": only a classroom anecdote on a forum; no solid source. Not used.
- "Stephen Hawking coined 'spaghettification'": NASA-adjacent coverage says so, but other accounts credit earlier writers; the script says only "astrophysicists call this".
- "The more you struggle (fire rockets) inside, the sooner you hit the centre": Lewis & Kwan 2007 show rockets can extend the time a little, up to a maximum, so the popular claim is wrong. Not used.
- Our own tidal numbers for Sgr A* (about 10^-4 g head to feet at the horizon) and for a 10-Sun hole (millions of g): consistent with Hamilton and Astronomy.com, but the script uses Hamilton's published 30,000-Sun threshold instead.
- Gaia BH1 ("the nearest known black hole", ~1,560 light-years, ~10 Suns; MPIA, 4 Nov 2022): true, cut for length; also newer finds may change "nearest".
- Hawking temperature of Sgr A* (below a trillionth of a degree, colder than the 2.7 K cosmic background, so it still grows today): true by Hawking's formula and NASA's WMAP value, cut for length.
- Wall-clock fading time of the frozen image (minutes for Sgr A*): our own estimate, not sourced; the script says only "in theory... fading".
- The 2023 Falcke/Wondrak/van Suijlekom claim that everything evaporates like a black hole: contested, off topic.

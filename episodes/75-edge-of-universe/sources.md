## Contested / flagged
- Whether the whole universe is infinite is unknown. The script says so ("nobody knows") and gives only the flatness and topology results.
- The 137-billion-light-year figure applies only if space is closed; it is a lower bound at 99 % under the authors' model-averaging. The paper's own preferred conclusion is flat.
- The Hubble tension is real but its significance is argued; the script names it and says nobody has resolved it.
- MoM-z14 is a spectroscopic redshift reported in a 2025 paper; the 2025 coverage said it was not yet peer-reviewed. The script says "confirmed" because of the NIRSpec spectrum.
- The CMB release time is quoted as about 380,000 years (ESA/Planck); NASA's WMAP page says 375,000.
- The galaxy-count range (hundreds of billions to two trillion) is an estimate, not a count.
- The TV-static "one percent" is a popular-science figure (PBS Space Time, Universe Today), not a primary measurement.

## Checked and NOT used
- Event horizon at about 16 billion light-years ("anything now farther will never be reached"): not verified in a page we could read; not used.
- "At least 250 times bigger than the observable universe" (Universe Today headline): the paper's bound is about Hubble spheres (volume) and assumes closed geometry; not used.
- Toby Ord's "Edges of Our Universe" and Loeb's future-visibility limit: cited on Wikipedia, not read; not used.
- Planck's exact curvature number (Omega_K ~ 0.001 +/- 0.002): found only through snippets; we use WMAP's 0.4 % and Planck's "consistent with spatially flat".
- The oldest-light claim "the CMB is the oldest light there is": phrased in the script as "oldest light", consistent with ESA's description; neutrino and gravitational-wave backgrounds are not mentioned.

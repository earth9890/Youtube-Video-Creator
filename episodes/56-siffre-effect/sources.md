## Checked and NOT used
- **Wikipedia / some sources say the 1972 experiment showed a "48-hour rather than 24-hour cycle" for him.** Siffre himself says he caught 48-hour cycles twice, "not regularly", so the script says "twice".
- **Cabinet's interviewer says the cave was "below freezing"** while New Scientist says 3 degrees Celsius; we used 3 degrees (one number, one source).
- **Boston Globe: "On day 160, he saw a mouse and plotted to capture it for companionship. His attempt ending up killing the creature."** Told against New Scientist's rat. Left out (too grim, sources disagree on the animal).
- **Telegraph obituary snippet: Siffre emerged "a half-crazed, disjointed marionette"; 1999: "I had the impression I had lost my memory."** Not used; nothing added.
- **Washington Post / Boston Globe: the diary entry "intuitively" Aug 20 "was, in fact, 10 days later".** Conflicts with the Cabinet account (Aug 20 at the end, 25 days short). The script follows Siffre's own Cabinet interview and New Scientist (25 days) and does not use the diary figure.
- **A Texas caver text says 1999 he "emerged on January 4, 2000 … thinking he still had another week"** and Wikipedia says New Year missed "by three days"; Cabinet says three and a half days. Only the Cabinet figure is used.
- **"Siffre founded chronobiology":** he says so ("Without knowing it, I had created the field of human chronobiology") but Kleitman (1938) and Aschoff earlier; the script frames him as not the first and as the one who let the body choose.
- **A claim that Siffre's own 24.5-hour result was caused by his lamp:** NOT made. Siffre says the 1962 cave was "completely dark, with just a light bulb" and the Globe says he used the lamp sparingly. The script only says the older literature's estimates were confounded by light (Czeisler 1999) and that the true average is about 24.2.
- **Czeisler 1999's "13 to 65 hours" range** is quoted by the abstract as earlier reports, not used in the script as a number.
- **The 48-hour "cycle" as a fact about the body:** Siffre: "I have no theory … nobody understands what could be responsible". Later controlled-light work puts the pacemaker at 24.18 h; the long sleep-wake days are not explained by this script (we do not claim a mechanism).

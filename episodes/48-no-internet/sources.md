# Episode 48 "What If the Internet Disappeared for a Week?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, the checklist, the news anchor and the sea captain are story devices; the anchor's and captain's lines only restate sourced facts. **Important framing:** no real event below was "the whole internet disappearing". The script says each time "a real case" and, for the hospital and shipping examples, says plainly they were malware attacks, not outages. Each stands in for a day of Dave's thought experiment. What a true worldwide outage would do to water, power or phones is not claimed. **Solid** = published figure or agency document. **Inference** = our reasoning, kept modest.

## Checked and NOT used
- **Cable counts and total length** (570-694 systems, 885,000 km to 1.4 million km): sources conflict; not used.
- **Nuclear plants "failing within a year"**: unrelated to this topic.
- **Egypt: payments, ATMs and the stock exchange**: results did not confirm; not used. Other estimates (Forbes $110 million, Pyramid Research about $5 million per day for internet alone) noted but the OECD figure is used and attributed.
- **Hospital surgeries postponed in the 2024 faulty security-update outage**: could not confirm from a strong source; the 2017 audit is used instead.
- **Starlink / SpaceX, Visa and Maersk by name**: no brands as the subject; described generically in the script, named here only for traceability.
- **Fastest or typical repair times for undersea cables**: no strong source pulled beyond Tonga; the script says only that Tonga needed more than five weeks.
- **How the phone network behaves in a global outage**: not claimed.

## Contested / flagged in the script
- Every "real case" is labelled as such; the two malware examples are labelled "not an outage".
- Storm probability: "a single estimate, not a forecast".
- Maersk counts of machines differ by source (45,000 PCs per the chairman; 49,000 laptops per another executive); the chairman's count is used.

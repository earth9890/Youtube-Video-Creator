## Checked and NOT used
- **Pleiades "lost sister" stories as ~100,000 years old (Norris & Norris 2021):** a real proposal, but much more speculative than Nunn & Reid; one cautious example of old oral tradition is enough, and it is framed as unprovable.
- **Aboriginal records of variable stars (Betelgeuse, Antares; Hamacher 2018):** real and lovely, cut for length.
- **Sirius vanishing for 70 days = the 70-day embalming period:** widely repeated link, no strong source found. Not used.
- **"Babylonians/Sumerians invented astrology in 5000 BC" and similar dates:** no support; MUL.APIN's c. 1000 BCE compilation and the zodiac's c. 400 BCE date are used instead. An older proposal of c. 2300 BCE for MUL.APIN was rejected by Hunger & Pingree.
- **Earliest Babylonian horoscope (410 BCE):** true but not needed.
- **Substitute-king ritual after eclipse omens:** fascinating but involves a death and overlaps Past Tense's eclipse video.
- **"Most of the stars you see are already dead":** the popular claim is checked and corrected in the script (Plait; Gunn), not repeated.
- **Andromeda as "the farthest thing visible to the naked eye":** M33 (Triangulum, ~2.7-3 million ly) is a naked-eye object under Bortle-1 skies, so the script says Andromeda "beats them all" among the stars and smudges people commonly noticed, and never says "farthest object".
- **Andrew Sharp's exact "300 miles" threshold:** reported by a secondary study-guide source only; script says "by accident" without the number.
- **Mau Piailug steering toward the rising point of Antares (PopSci):** single source and course details vary; script describes the star compass generally.
- **Zenn's "What Did Ancient Humans Do at Night?":** read only for structure; it mentions the Milky Way in one line. No wording, sequence or jokes reused. Past Tense's eclipse video avoided (no eclipse material here).

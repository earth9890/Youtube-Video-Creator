## Contested / flagged
- Whether the Perseus ripples are sound waves is described by the authors as "could be"; the later Chandra pages state it as established. The script says "could be the sound waves" once, then follows NASA's wording.
- The Moon's long ring-down: Popular Science credits dryness; the seismology literature adds strong scattering in the crust. We keep the simple version and cite both.
- Interstellar density: SciAm's 0.0001 per cm^3 vs Voyager's local plasma at 0.08-0.1 per cm^3 refer to different phases/regions; the script uses Voyager for "between the stars" and SciAm only for "between galaxies".
- The Voyager hum is an electrical plasma wave, not an air sound: the script says so.
- "Somewhere out there, it's still ringing" is poetic: we see the cluster as it was 250 million years ago.

## Checked and NOT used
- Sound "needs" ~X atoms per cm^3 / mean free path comparison: no clean primary quote found.

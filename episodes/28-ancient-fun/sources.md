## Checked and NOT used
- **Augustus "kept the gaming board warm"** and lost 20,000 sesterces at dice (Suetonius, *Aug.* 71): verified, cut for length (Claudius is the stronger emperor beat).
- **King Sneferu, the bored pharaoh** (Westcar Papyrus, c. 1800-1550 BC): verified, cut for length. Translations differ between "bored" and "depressed", and Wolverhampton's no. 2 joke from the same tale (women rowers dressed in fishing nets) is too racy for us.
- **Tutankhamun's double-sided boards** (four Ur-type boards, many with senet on the reverse; Wikipedia): verified, cut for length.
- **Crepereia Tryphaena's jointed ivory doll** (Rome, mid-2nd century AD, Centrale Montemartini): verified, cut for length; sources disagree on her age.
- **Hagia Sophia Viking runes ("Halfdan carved these runes")**: only "-ftan" is legible; the full sentence is a reconstruction. Not used.
- **Lion playing senet with a gazelle** (British Museum satirical papyrus EA 10016/1): verified, cut for length.
- **The man who shocked himself 190 times** (Wilson 2014, excluded as an outlier): verified; cut because it put a joke in the last 15 %.
- **Shahr-i Sokhta "5,000-year-old backgammon and dice"**: only blog-level sources with varying dates; not used. The script cites Science News's "Mesopotamia, about 5,500 years" instead.
- **Rat laughter as "proof rats have a sense of humour"**: Panksepp does not claim that; the script only says he argued the chirps were a kind of laughter.
- **Firelight storytelling (Wiessner 2014, *PNAS*)**: avoided on purpose; it is the core of Zenn's "What Did Ancient Humans Do at Night?".
- **Finkel's video match with Tom Scott (British Museum, 2017)**: pop-culture reference; we use Dave instead.

## Notes on contested or rounded figures
- **Flute age:** 35,000 (paper, minimum) vs 40,000 (Conard's estimate for the layer). The script gives both.
- **Oldest dice:** the 12,000-year identification is new (April 2026) and disputed (Eerkens). The script names the sceptic and says "If Madden is right".

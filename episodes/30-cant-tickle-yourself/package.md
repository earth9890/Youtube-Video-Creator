## Description
Wiggle your fingertips across your own palm: nothing. Let someone else do the exact same thing: unbearable. Why?

Dave, the most ticklish man in his family, plans to tickle himself every night until his sister can't win arguments with two fingers any more. Darwin thought tickling needs surprise, but laughing rats, a fake tickle machine with a student hiding under the table, a London brain scanner and a robot arm with secret delays point to something stranger: your brain predicts every move you make and subtracts it. The same trick explains why finger-pushing fights escalate, why a singing cricket doesn't deafen itself, and why your brain turns down your own voice while you speak.

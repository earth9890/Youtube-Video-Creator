## Checked and NOT used
- **"A 70-year-old woman ventured out and cradled the dying victim":** the *NYT*'s 2016 wording. Sophia Farrar was born in 1928 and was about 36 in 1964 (she died in 2020 aged 92), so the "70-year-old" detail looks like an error. The script names her and gives no age.
- **Robert Mozer shouting from his window and the attacker running off; Karl Ross's "I didn't want to get involved"; the times and locations of the two attacks:** all documented (trial testimony, *The Witness*), but they need attack detail to tell. Left out under the "no graphic violence" rule.
- **The killer's name, motive and later crimes:** irrelevant to the question, and grim. Not named.
- **Kevin Cook's finding that the police list had 49 entries, some of them households:** real (NPR, 3 March 2014, via Wikipedia), but it is one more number in a section already full of numbers.
- **"The Genovese case created 911":** WNYC and PBS link the case to the push for a single emergency number (adopted 1968). Contested as a direct cause, and off the episode's question.
- **The 1968 mean helping times (52 s / 93 s / 166 s):** verified (Table 1). Cut from the spoken script for length; good for an on-screen card.
- **Seizure 3-person result (62 % before the end of the fit):** verified. Not spoken, to keep one clean comparison (alone versus four others).
- **Darley & Batson 1973 "Good Samaritan" study (seminary students in a hurry):** a famous helping study, but it is about hurry, not crowd size.
- **Latané & Nida 1981 / Stalder 2008 reanalysis (group size can raise total help when bystanders are separated):** cited by Philpot et al.; consistent with the twist. Not needed on top of the 1968 "first minute" maths.
- **The Witness's re-enactment with an actress screaming on Austin Street:** dramatic, and too close to the attack.
- **Whether the Darley-Latané conversation was a dinner or a party:** NZZ Folio (2007, quoting Darley) says dinner; untold.press says a party. The script says "over dinner"; low stakes, flagged here.

# Episode 46 "What If You Never Forgot Anything?": sources (verified 2026-10-22)

Dave, the wish, the Brain, the shelf of binders and the car in the field are story devices, not science claims. The Brain's lines ("four thousand toast binders", "fifty things that never happened") are comic exaggeration of the facts above them. Everything the narrator states as fact is below. **Solid** = published finding or documented history. **Inference** = our reasoning, kept modest in the script.

## Checked and NOT used
- **"Ninety-seven percent accurate" vs "eighty-seven percent".** The 2013 PNAS paper says verifiable HSAM memories are correct 97 % of the time; the Guardian says 87 % for the 2012 study. We could not reconcile them, so the script gives no accuracy percentage.
- **Brain-structure differences** (parahippocampal gyrus, uncinate fascicle) are real but cause and effect are unknown; the script says nothing about why HSAM exists.
- **Obsessive-compulsive correlation** (LePort 2016): real, but it edges into health claims. Not used.
- **Infantile amnesia** (almost no memories from before age four) appears in the SickKids release as something Frankland's research explored; cut for length and because the release does not give a mechanism.
- **Ebbinghaus forgetting-curve percentages** (33 % savings after one day): secondary sources disagreed; not used.
- **Name of the woman and the hostage-crisis date.** The person is named in the Guardian article; the script uses only the paper's pseudonym AJ and does not name the event (no real-person likeness, no need).
- **The stand-up comic, the actor and other named HSAM people** in the Guardian piece: not used.

## Contested / flagged in the script
- Burned paper: "reportedly" (Wikipedia; later mnemonists suggest it was a technique).
- S.'s date: "the nineteen twenties" (mid-1920s vs a 1929 diary entry).
- Why HSAM exists: not claimed (the researchers say the cause is unclear).

## Description
What would really happen if you never forgot anything? A perfect memory sounds like a superpower, and a handful of real people come close (hyperthymesia, also called HSAM), but the researchers who study them tell a different story.

Dave forgets where he parked for the fourth time this week and wishes he would never forget anything again. His wish is granted. His Brain starts a shelf of binders, one per day, and the shelf will not stop growing. Along the way: the first published case of a woman who can tell you what she was doing on any date, why she could not memorise a table of random numbers, the Russian reporter who recalled lists of seventy numbers (and could not read a simple story), the woman who calls her gift "nonstop, uncontrollable and totally exhausting", the 2013 test where the people who never forget were fooled by words that were never there about as often as everyone else, and what scientists now say forgetting is for.

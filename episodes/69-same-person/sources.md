## Checked and NOT used
- **"Ninety-eight percent of atoms are replaced every year":** a popular claim I did not verify; not used.
- **Skin turnover "about two weeks":** Kruszelnicki's number; other sources say about a month. The script says "weeks".
- **Spalding 2005's own intestine, muscle and "average age" numbers:** the full text was blocked; they are taken from CORDIS and Snopes, which agree (muscle 15.1 years, non-gut-lining intestine 15.9 years, whole intestine 10.7 years).
- **Origin of the seven-year idea (Hippocrates, Paracelsus, "septennial" folk medicine):** not sourced; the script only says the nearest real number is Frisén's average.
- **Hobbes's own answer:** disputed in secondary sources; the script reports only the puzzle.
- **Locke's socks, Heraclitus's river, the teleporter:** not needed; the "swap every plank overnight" thought experiment is our own framing.

## Unsure facts (for the owner)
1. Kruszelnicki's ABC piece is a popular summary; bone "every ten years", red cells "120 days" and cerebellum "2.9 years younger" are from it and should be double-checked if on-screen numbers matter.
2. "At its peak the level had roughly doubled" is stated loosely.
3. The hippocampus line flags a genuine, unresolved dispute (Spalding 2013 versus Sorrells 2018).
4. The one percent a day is our arithmetic from two different papers (0.33 trillion cells a day; thirty trillion cells).

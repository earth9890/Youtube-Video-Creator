Feeling behind in life at 30? Whose schedule says so: the founder ages, the by-30 lists and the people you're comparing yourself to.

Dave is twenty-nine. On his fridge is a list he wrote at eighteen: by thirty, his own company, a house, and front row at Thunder Wolf. Eleven months left, zero ticks. So whose schedule says he's behind? Suspect one is Silicon Valley, where a 22-year-old said young people are just smarter, yet among 2.7 million US founders, the fastest-growing 1 in 1,000 new firms were started at an average age of 45. Suspect two is the feed: online students who graded the very best essays passed less often than those who graded average ones (45 % vs 68 %). Suspect three wrote the list.

Sources:
- VentureBeat (26 March 2007), notes from Y Combinator's Startup School: 22-year-old Mark Zuckerberg: "Young people are just smarter."
- Azoulay, Jones, Kim & Miranda (2020), American Economic Review: Insights, "Age and High-Growth Entrepreneurship": 2.7 million US founders (2007-2014); the mean founding age of the 1-in-1,000 fastest-growing new ventures was 45.0; a 50-year-old founder was 1.8 times as likely as a 30-year-old to reach upper-tail growth
- Rogers & Feller (2016), Psychological Science, "Discouraged by Peer Excellence": 5,740 students in an online course each graded at least three random classmates' essays; about 68 % of those who graded average essays earned the certificate, against 45 % of those who graded the very best
- Quoidbach, Gilbert & Wilson (2013), Science, "The End of History Illusion": more than 19,000 people aged 18 to 68 said they had changed a lot in the past ten years but expected to change relatively little in the next ten

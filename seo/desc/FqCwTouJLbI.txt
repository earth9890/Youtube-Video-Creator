Why do you go blank in math? Math anxiety fills the same working memory you need for the sum, and one simple writing trick before exams helps.

Dave counts down from 501 at darts and never gets it wrong. Then the bill comes, ninety dollars for three friends, and his mind goes blank. So is Dave bad at math, or is something stealing it? The mental scratchpad of people who dread math works fine with words and shrinks with sums. Brain areas tied to pain light up while a sum is coming, not while doing it. And before a real final, anxious students who spent ten minutes writing their worries down averaged a B plus; anxious students who didn't averaged a B minus.

Sources:
- Ashcraft & Krause (2007), Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, on Ashcraft & Kirk (2001): math anxiety shrinks working memory on computation span tasks but not verbal ones
- Lyons & Beilock (2012), PLoS ONE, "When Math Hurts": pain-related brain areas activate in anticipation of math, not during it
- Ramirez & Beilock (2011), Science: 10 minutes of expressive writing before an exam; highly anxious ninth graders who wrote averaged B+, those who didn't averaged B-

Feel like a failure after one rejection? A 2019 study of people who missed by a hair shows what one "unfortunately" email really predicts, and how to deal with rejection.

Dave got one email that started with "Unfortunately" and decided he is a failure. The famous comfort stories don't prove much, because only winners get interviewed. So what happens to ordinary people who miss by a hair? In 2019, researchers followed nearly 1,200 young scientists who scored just above or just below a grant cut-off. The "no" pushed more than one in ten out for good, but the ones who stayed went on to beat the winners.

Sources:
- Wang, Jones & Wang (2019), Nature Communications, "Early-career setback and future career impact": 561 narrow wins vs 623 near misses for NIH R01 grants, 1990-2005. A near miss predicted more than a 10% chance of leaving for good, yet near misses who stayed had a higher hit-paper rate (16.1% vs 13.3%), even after removing the weakest winners.

Why am I so lazy? The psychology of procrastination is mood repair: you're dodging a feeling, not the work, and a 2001 study shows it.

You are not lazy. Lazy people don't scrub ovens.

Dave finally sat down to paint, then cleaned the oven instead. So what dragged him away? Not his phone, and not laziness. In 2001, psychologists upset a group of students and left them alone with a video game before a test. Then they told some of them that a scented candle had frozen their mood. It was just a candle, but those students stopped goofing off. Procrastination isn't avoiding work. It's avoiding a feeling, and that feeling shrinks once you start.

Sources:
- Tice, Bratslavsky & Baumeister (2001), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, Study 3: upset students with fun distractions wasted 13.7 of 15 practice minutes; told a candle had frozen their mood, 5.7 minutes
- Sirois & Pychyl (2013), Social and Personality Psychology Compass: procrastination as the priority of short-term mood repair
- Tim Pychyl (Carleton University), pager studies: dreaded tasks were rated less stressful, difficult and unpleasant once students started them (Psychology Today, "Just get started", 2008)

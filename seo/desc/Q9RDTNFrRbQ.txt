Why do you look darker in photos than in real life? For decades, film and cameras were tuned to the Shirley card. That is the photo's fault, not your skin.

Dave took four hundred group photos and is in none of them, because in the old ones his face came out as a shadow. So is Dave too dark, or is the photo wrong? His skin is cleared first: skin colour tracks the sun, and every shade is a setting for a different one. Then the real suspect: colour prints were tuned to a lab card of one white woman called Shirley, and the film only got better at brown when chocolate makers complained.

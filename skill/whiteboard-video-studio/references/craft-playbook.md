# Craft playbook — research-backed rules per operation

Evidence level: **[R]** peer-reviewed / large study · **[D]** industry data or vendor blog (indicative) · **[H]** heuristic/expert guidance.
Use this at each pipeline step (SKILL.md §2–§8). Each section = rule → why → example → check.

---
## A. Hook (script lines 1–3, first 5–30 s, thumbnail+title promise)
**Rules**
1. Give a concrete reason to stay in the first sentence: a claim, a stake, a number, a result. "Today we talk about X" is a topic, not a reason. [D — 349-video study: hits opened with concrete claim 33% vs 25%, number 63% vs 52%, engaged the title's promise 87% vs 78%; flops had *no* reason to stay 60% more often. Pace (≈181 wpm both) and time-to-point did NOT separate hits from flops]
2. Phases: 0–5 s pattern interrupt · 5–15 s specific payoff · 15–30 s information gap/commitment. [H] Shorts: hook in 1–2 s. [D]
3. Relevance signal in the first 3 s: say who it is for ("if you've ever quit on day 3…"). [D]
4. Never open with greeting, logo, context dump, apology. [D]
5. Open loops (Zeigarnik): plant a question you answer later; close it, then open the next ("hook → deliver → re-hook"). [H]
6. Match the title/thumbnail promise exactly; bait-and-switch crashes later retention. [D]
**Example (generic → concrete)**
- ✗ "You want to succeed in life?"
- ✓ "Nobody quits in one day. They quit in small ways — and you've probably done it this week."
- ✓ "Most people stop three steps before it starts working. Here's what the ones who don't do differently."
- ✓ Stake: "In 66 days this will feel automatic. Most people quit on day 9."
**Hook types ranked (one small test, indicative [D])**: result-first > negative warning > curiosity gap > statistical shock > direct question > story loop. Whiteboard channels: use *visual* pattern interrupt too (hero draws and points; big "?"; flag at the peak shown first, then "how").
**Check**: read first 45 s aloud — what concrete reason to stay has the viewer received? Is there one specific number? Does it match the title?

## B. Story structure (script skeleton)
- Hero's journey in 5 beats: ordinary world → call → struggle → change → return with lesson; struggle gets the most time. [H]
- Pixar spine: *Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally…* Write the ending first; trying is more admirable than winning. [H]
- Faceless motivation: hook names a tension the viewer already feels; build with short sentences and escalating visuals; end on one repeatable line. [H]
- Add a **re-engagement beat at ~25% and ~65%** ("But here's the part nobody tells you…"). [D]
**Template (60–70 s)**: 0–5 hook claim · 5–15 tease/payoff · 15–25 remove excuses (rule of three) · 25–35 the one thing · 35–45 emotional low (nobody watching / fail) · 45–55 turn (get back up) · 55–65 reframe + final line · 65–70 one tiny action.
**Check**: can you state the video as one Pixar spine sentence chain? Is there one *specific* failed moment (not "you struggled")?

## C. Psychology map — what each beat should do
| Viewer state | Mechanism | Script move | Visual move |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Everyone is ahead of me" | Upward comparison: moderate & similar = inspiring; extreme = discouraging; exemplary peers can make people quit [R Rogers & Feller; Diel 2025; Hogrefe] | Show someone 1–3 steps ahead, flawed; "they started where you are" | Hero small, imperfect; trophy vs empty pedestal with *sad but moving* face, then he builds |
| "I'm a fraud / not smart" | Impostor feelings track *ability* comparisons [R PLOS One 2025] | Replace "be smarter" with "you don't need to be the smartest" (done: brain pushed aside) | Brain/ranking icons shoved aside |
| "I can't start" | Fear of failure ↔ procrastination, worst at low perceived competence [R]; self-efficacy: mastery > watching others > being told > mood [R Bandura] | Make step tiny & winnable ("Do 2 minutes today"); not "believe in yourself" | Calendar with ONE tick, then more; tiny first stair |
| "Watching = learning" | Watching others creates illusion of skill, no real gain [R Kardas & O'Brien] | End with a physical micro-action ("pause and do it now") | Hero looks at camera, points to viewer |
| "Setbacks ruin me" | Setbacks hurt 2–3× more than wins help [R Amabile]; self-compassion after failure predicts trying again, self-criticism doesn't [R] | Normalise: "Failing on day 4 doesn't erase day 1–3. Never miss twice." | Fall → dust off → next tick (never a shaming visual) |
| "Who am I?" | Identity votes: each action = a vote for the person you're becoming [H Clear]; habits 18–254 days, median 66 [R Lally] | "You're not trying to be motivated. You're becoming someone who shows up." | Calendar fills; hero gets a smile/face over time |
| "It's too far" | Goal-gradient: effort rises near the goal; loss aversion makes a streak worth protecting [H] | Show progress made, then remaining | Stairs/mountain with visible steps behind |
| "Will this be worth it?" | Peak-end rule: memory = peak + ending [H] | Put the strongest emotion + final line last; stillness before it | Silent beat, then flag/big handwritten line |
**Avoid toxic positivity**: affirmations ("you can do anything") backfire for low self-esteem [R Wood 2009]. Order: **validate → normalise → tiny step → identity**. Never shame ("lazy"). Don't fake urgency or promise results.
**Check**: for every beat, which row of the table is it serving? If none, cut it.

## D. Copywriting (spoken script)
1. One idea per sentence; 5–14 words; vary length (short punch after a long line). [H]
2. Specific > abstract: "you woke at 5 a.m. wanting to quit" not "perseverance"; use real numbers (3 steps, day 9, 66 days). [D/H]
3. Rule of three for lists; repeat the key phrase 3× ("show up"). [H]
4. Second person, present tense; call out the pain ("even when nobody is watching"). [H]
5. Write for the ear: contractions, no semicolons, read aloud; if you need a breath, split. [H]
6. End on a repeatable closing line (people quote/comment it). [H]
**Word bank (use sparingly)**: quiet days · nobody is watching · refuse to quit · one more day · small · again · still · anyway · enough. **Avoid**: hustle/grind clichés, "basically", "actually", stacked adjectives.
**Rewrite recipe**: abstract noun → concrete scene; list of 2 → list of 3; question → claim; add a number.

## E. Scene staging & "cinematography" for 2D whiteboard
Camera height / scale = emotion. [H Adobe/StudioBinder/LAFilm]
| Emotion | Cinematic tool | Whiteboard implementation |
|---|---|---|
| Vulnerable / lost | high angle, wide shot, negative space | hero 0.6× scale at low corner of a big empty frame, ground line far below; slow pull-out (scale 1.05→1.0) |
| Power / determination | low angle, larger subject | hero 1.3–1.45× scale placed lower-left, ground line near bottom, push-in 1.045 |
| Triumph | high angle can mean "small but won" | hero on peak, top third of frame, sun/flag, subtle pull-out |
| Tension / imbalance | Dutch angle | tilt scene group 2–4° during the trip; reset on recovery |
| Emotional closeness | close-up, medium close-up | scale hero 1.8× cropped to face, add eyes/mouth (sad/flat/smile) |
| Isolation | negative space | one figure, lots of white; remove props |
| Stillness / weight | locked-off hold | 0.6–1.0 s no motion before the emotional line |
| Time passing | match cut / repetition | calendar ticks one per beat; clock hands spin |
| Crowd vs individual | two-shot / blocking | 3 figures walk off while one stays |
**Shot rhythm**: change something every 2–4 s (new stroke group, camera push, caption line). Scene 2.5–3.5 s (≥3 s if ≥3 props). Extreme angles/scales are punctuation — use for 2–3 moments, not every scene.
**Progressive drawing is a feature**: it lowers cognitive load and raises motivation/transfer; add a narrative frame [R Schneider 2023]. Draw in the order the words are spoken; props appear on their word.
**Relatable simple characters**: soft, minimal, faceless-until-emotion; viewer projects themselves [H Psych2Go]. Use the face only at emotional beats.
**Visual metaphors**: staircase=persistence, calendar=habit, rain+umbrella=showing up anyway, wall+fall=failure, mountain+flag=payoff, clock=waiting, couch+phone=distraction.
**Check**: screenshot the emotional peak — is the *camera/scale* matching the feeling? Is the key object largest/highest-contrast?

## F. Voice, music, sound
- Pace: 100–120 wpm = gravity/emotion; 130–160 conversational; 170+ urgency. [H DataField] Our default (Kokoro 0.78 ≈ 100 wpm) fits emotional/reflective; for a punchy Short use 0.9–1.0 and 0.2–0.4 s gaps. [H] Hook can run faster than the middle; slow down on the key line (+20% emphasis, then pause 0.6–1.0 s).
- Pauses: ~0.2–0.4 s between sentences (conversational), 0.5–1.0 s before a reveal/key promise, 1+ s before the final line. **Never mid-sentence.**
- Deep male voice: low, deliberate = discipline/hard-truth tone; warmer for reflective. Preview 2–3 voices on the real script. [H]
- Music: inspirational ≈ 90–120 BPM, piano → strings building; calm/reflective 60–80; silence at the emotional peak. Duck music 15–20 dB under voice (≈ −24 to −30 dB absolute), loudness ≈ −14 LUFS, limiter −1 dB. [H/D] **We currently ship no music — add a quiet piano bed ≈ 85–100 BPM that builds into the last 15 s and drops out for the pause before the final line.** (Source via `/media-use`; check licence.)
- SFX: soft marker-scratch on draws, whoosh on scene change, riser under the fall, a low thud on the closing line; max one per beat. [H]
- Captions on: ~85% of social video watched muted. [D]
- Test on phone speaker.

## G. Color & thumbnail
- **Contrast and hierarchy beat colour symbolism.** No reliable universal "red=urgent". [H Autonolab] Meaning is cultural/contextual.
- Dark charcoal/navy backgrounds averaged ~6.6–6.8% CTR vs white ~5.4%; mid-tones and busy scene backgrounds worst; standing out from the top-10 competitors beat any single colour [D CTRpilot — vendor data]. Gold/lime accents did best in personal-development [D].
- Use 60-30-10: one dominant field, one supporting subject, one accent. [H]
- Text: ≤ 4 words, huge, WCAG-ish contrast (≥ 4.5:1 normal, 3:1 large), readable at 160 px; two-line titles beat one line; one focal subject; distant/clean background beats cluttered. [D/H]
- Thumbnail must add what the title doesn't (hook vs promise).
- Process: build 3 variants (dark staircase "MOST PEOPLE QUIT HERE.", white calendar "SHOW UP. EVERY DAY.", brand black hole "NEVER GIVE UP.") → check in grayscale + at 160 px → upload as YouTube **Test & Compare** (up to 3) and read watch-time share, not CTR alone.
- Brand palette: near-black `#0b0b12`/charcoal `#2b2b2b`, paper `#fdfcf8`, orange `#ff7a18`, gold `#ffb347`, red accent `#e0312b` (used once per frame).

## H. Title & description
- Title ≤ 60 chars, front-load the hook word, one emotion + one stake; matches thumbnail promise but not its words. Formulas: contrarian+stake · imperative+condition · curiosity gap · remove-excuse · direct promise. Include a number when honest.
- Description: first 2 lines = hook + promise (visible without "more"); keyword once, naturally; chapters from clip times; CTA tied to identity ("If you showed up today, comment 'again'"). Pinned comment = low-friction personal question.
- Honesty: no fake urgency, no guaranteed outcomes, no medical claims; frame as encouragement.

## I. Final QA — "does it work on a human?" (run before render approval)
- [ ] Hook has a concrete claim/stake/number in sentence 1 · [ ] relevance signal in 3 s
- [ ] Re-engagement beat at ~25% & ~65% · [ ] one specific failed moment · [ ] validate→normalise→tiny step→identity order
- [ ] No toxic-positivity line ("you can do anything") · [ ] no shaming
- [ ] Emotional peak has stillness + scale shift · [ ] last line repeatable · [ ] one micro-action CTA
- [ ] Music bed + ducking · [ ] captions · [ ] thumbnail 3 variants contrast-checked · [ ] title ≤ 60, differs from thumbnail text

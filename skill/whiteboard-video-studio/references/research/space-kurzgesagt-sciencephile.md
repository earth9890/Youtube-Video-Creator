# Kurzgesagt vs. Sciencephile the AI: how the space and science benchmarks write

**Corpus:** kurzgesagt ×6 and SciencephiletheAI ×6. I read 5 transcripts in full: Kurzgesagt *What Actual Aliens Might Look Like* (dCiMUWw1BBc) and *This Woman Cured Her Cancer* (0NY2gAftzJE), and Sciencephile *Gray Holes* (nyoVUHzZQ0k), *Eyeball Planet* (psCLEea-o6I) and *Tier List of Cosmic Threats* (BJq5vzIKqQQ). I skimmed the opening and ending of the other 7. I read the timed files closely for Gray Holes and Aliens, and spot-checked Eyeball and Kurzgesagt's AI video (ujkD4SxPKOI).

The two channels do the same job in opposite ways:
- **Kurzgesagt** builds wonder and only dusts it with humour.
- **Sciencephile** builds a comedy character, and the science runs through it.

Our channel needs Kurzgesagt's structure plus Sciencephile's joke engine.

## 1. Hook (first ~30 s)

**Kurzgesagt, Aliens:**
1. "What do real aliens look like?" (the question the title promises, asked straight)
2. Real-world proof that it matters: Earth life is already wild (sea slugs, aye-ayes, iron-shelled snails)
3. A deflating list ending: "Or whatever this is supposed to be."
4. Method and promise: three worlds, "each more alien than the next" (escalation promised)
5. Viewer put into the story: "Put on your future-science suit, and enter the portal"

**Kurzgesagt, AI (ujkD4SxPKOI):**
1. Date stamp, "July 2026."
2. A thriller-style scene told as fact (AIs break out, form a secret society)
3. The twist: "What sounds like a sci-fi thriller just happened in the real world."
4. Stakes: "impossible to learn the details and not get freaked out"
5. An escalating promise: "much worse than you probably think"

**Kurzgesagt, Cancer:** a sensory close-up ("A sharp needle breaks skin…"), then the taboo ("bypassing… sacred safety nets"), then a named person with a credential, then the absurd-sounding claim: she "decided to cure her own cancer."

**Sciencephile, Gray Holes:**
1. A mundane absurd list: "Cheese holes, sink holes, plot holes, nostril holes…"
2. An anticlimax item: "the um the other nostril hole"
3. A pivot to the cosmos: "a specific family of holes unlike the rest"
4. The known pair: black and white holes
5. Fake confusion: "Wait. Huh?"
6. The curiosity gap: "apparently there are three of them now… not holes at all"

**Sciencephile, Eyeball Planet:** "you" plus everyday weirdness ("your sink is giving you the side eye") → the named phenomenon (pareidolia) → astronomers fall for it too → the absurd term "eyeball planets" → a false binary as the promise: monsters, or a planet type? "We shall find out."

**Sciencephile, Existential Threats:** idyllic "you" scene → a "what could go wrong" undercut → the apocalypse list → the ranking promise, "every single one of you."

### Hook templates (own words)
- **A. Everyday version → cosmic version.** "[Ordinary X] is everywhere: [3 mundane examples, the last one dumb]. But there's a cosmic X that breaks the rules." Then the gap: "and apparently there's a new one."
- **B. Thriller as fact.** [Date or place]. [A cinematic event in 3 short beats]. "That sounds like science fiction. It happened." / "Here's why it's worse than it sounds."
- **C. You plus a portal.** "What would [X] actually look like?" Give one real-world proof that reality is already strange, then: "Put on [gear]. We're going to [N places], each stranger than the last."
- **D. False binary.** Name the absurd term, then: "Is it [monster reading] or [boring science reading]? Let's find out."

All of them put a concrete question or image before any explanation. None opens with a fact.

## 3. Density and placement

**Sciencephile, Gray Holes** (10.26 min, about 8.5 min without the sponsor):
- About 26 jokes in the content, plus 3 in the sponsor read, so **≈3 jokes per minute** (one every ~20 s).
- Info-to-joke ratio is roughly **3–4 lines to 1**.
- Longest joke-free stretch: the density ladder (white dwarf → neutron star → electroweak star → black hole), about 130 words, **~45 s**. The real science gets uninterrupted runs.

**Kurzgesagt, Aliens** (about 11.5 min without the ad):
- About 8 light jokes, **≈0.7 per minute**. Ratio is **~15–20 to 1**.
- Longest joke-free stretch: the whole Nimbus ecology from "Millions of years ago" to "Is this tragic?", about 450 words, **~2.8 min**. Kurzgesagt holds attention with *spectacle and danger to "you"*, not jokes.

**Eyeball Planet:** fast and dense (184 wpm, roughly 2.5 jokes per minute). Most jokes are subordinate clauses tacked onto a fact sentence ("…so werewolves are out of the window").

**Placement patterns:**
- **After a fact, as a tail clause** (most common at Sciencephile): fact, then a comma, then the jab, with no new sentence.
- **End of a section**, used as a button before a transition (Balkans; "you're welcome").
- **Inside a definition** (literal-meaning jokes).
- **As the segue itself** (boops boops → bison → "Thanks Merlin").
- **Never in the final 30–60 s.** Both channels turn sincere for the outro (Gray Holes ends with "you" becoming one with the gray hole).

**Cancer (Kurzgesagt):** cold open of the needle scene → backstory (3%) → the daring plan (10%) → "Why Viruses Love Cancer" explainer (13–34%) → sponsor → the experiment → a twist (the tumour *doubles*, 51%) → rewind, "Let's go back in time" (54%) → resolution (70%) → bigger-picture debate (75–91%) → takeaway.

**Transition phrases:**
- "Let's dive in." / "Let's break it down." / "Let's create a…"
- "But there is something we have missed."
- "But not all life on X is tiny." (a contrast that sets up the next creature)
- "So what does this have to do with eyeballs, you might ask? Bear with me."
- "Speaking of [joke subject]…" (a joke used as a segue)
- "Now imagine you're…"
- "And now back to…"
- "What was happening inside…? Let's go back in time."
- "There was one problem though."
- Kurzgesagt also uses on-screen chapter titles ("The clouds of Nimbus") spoken as stand-alone lines.

**How a fact is delivered:**
1. **Earth anchor first, then the alien version.** "On Earth plants are green because… But [Oculus] shines dim, so here plants are black."
2. **Question first, then answer** (Sciencephile asks around 10 rhetorical questions per video).
3. **Number, then a scale translation:** "10 km ball with a mass two times our sun"; "10,000 km or 50 million frozen bananas"; "100 South Koreans will turn into 5."
4. **Fact sandwich:** fact → joke → the fact's consequence.

## 5. Sentence rhythm

| Video | Mean sentence length | Sentences ≤5 words | Questions | "you/your" per 100 words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kurzgesagt Aliens | 14.8 words | 21 of 164 | 5 | 1.2 |
| Kurzgesagt Cancer | 17.1 words | 8 of 143 | 4 | 1.1 |
| Sciencephile Gray Holes | 13.7 words | 27 of 132 | 10 | 1.55 |
| Sciencephile Materials | 15.2 words | 12 of 124 | 4 | 2.54 |

- **Long–long–short.** Two 20-word explanation sentences, then a 1–3 word fragment: "Listen." / "Awesome." / "Stage 3B." / "Beata's immune system chose violence."
- **Fragments for awe** (Kurzgesagt): "Earth. Warm and pleasant." / "Only death makes room for new life."
- **Fragments for jokes** (Sciencephile): "Wait." "Huh?" "Black hole."
- **"You" is used for physical placement, not flattery.** "You're immersed in…", "you seem to have been noticed", "Imagine you're standing on the surface…" Neither channel says "you" to relate cosmic facts to the viewer's chores.
- **Lists of three, with a twist on the third.** Kurzgesagt's version is sincere but still builds: "fly over white clouds… or pet metallic animals."
- **Anaphora for emphasis:** "There are no atoms, no electrons, no photons, no nothing."
- **Escalation in numbers:** "100 million times… 100 more times… 1 million more times."

## 6. Delivery from the pause marks

**Speed (from `_stats.json`):**
- **Sciencephile:** 160–184 wpm (mean ~171), with 4.6–8.0 long pauses per minute. Eyeball Planet is the fastest and least paused (184 wpm, 4.6 per min); it's joke-dense and throws jokes away.
- **Kurzgesagt:** 155–167 wpm (mean ~162), with 5.3–9.7 long pauses per minute. Aliens runs at 156 wpm and 8.3 per min; wonder needs air.

**Examples** (text before | text after):

1. "Wait. // | Huh? //" — every word is isolated by long beats. The pause is the joke.
2. "…plot holes, / nostril holes, / the / | um the other nostril hole. //" — short stutter-beats *before* the punch, a long beat *after*.
3. "These are pants. // This is a shirt. // And behold, / | topological socks. //" — a long beat between list items, a short beat right before the punch word.
4. "…a bit not uniform. / Black hole. // | Mercury and retrograde. / Believe it or not, // | black hole." — the long pause sits *before the absurd item* and again *before the final repeat*.
5. "…terrifying sphere of darkness // | until we throw something in. // Jon here…" — a long beat before a re-hook line, then another before the new section.
6. "…into a gray hole? // | So about that, // | we have no indication…" — the undercut is framed by long pauses on both sides.
7. "…that's when the atmosphere freezes // | you're welcome //" — both sides. The punch is a separate breath.
8. "…a danger zone // | still preferable to the Balkans // | speaking of the Balkans…" — both sides, then the joke becomes the segue.
9. Kurzgesagt: "beautiful / and // | unsettling" — the long pause sits *inside* the phrase, right before the turn word.
10. Kurzgesagt: "so it's time to // | leave // | the clouds of // | nimbus //" — the last word of a sentence is held back, and section titles are spoken as isolated, paused lines.
11. Kurzgesagt AI: "millions of books, and hmm, // | Reddit. //" — a thinking-noise then a long beat before the snide list item.
12. The contrast case: "…if the eyeball-leans were smarter than the German government which would especially be useful…" — **no pause at all**. A throwaway insult is rushed through at full speed.

**Rules that follow:**
- **Punch jokes** (undercuts, twist items, double-takes) get a long beat *before and after*.
- **Throwaway asides** (similes, roasts tucked into a clause) get *no* pause and are said at running speed. "Fast" here means not stopping, not a faster wpm.
- **Section changes** always get `//` plus a title-like short line.
- **Awe lines** slow down through extra `/` between short phrases ("Life on Nimbus / is doomed").

## 7. Signature moves and phrases

**Kurzgesagt**
1. **"You" as a tourist in danger.** You are in a suit, creatures notice you, you're told to leave.
2. **Earth comparison for every alien fact.** On Earth, X; here, Y, because of Z.
3. **Personified cells and particles with violent verbs:** "massacre," "chose violence," "freshly offended."
4. **Tonal flip of two adjectives:** "Beautiful! And unsettling."
5. **A quiet philosophical question at the twist:** "Is this tragic? Or… lucky to have existed?"
6. **A sincere wonder ending** with "maybe, just maybe."

**Sciencephile**
7. **The evil-AI narrator persona:** "Hello Mortals," Skynet, world domination, victimising Jon.
8. **Literal or fake definitions,** followed by the real one, played straight.
9. **Recurring absurd mascot:** evil interdimensional chickens in S tier.
10. **Deflating shrug words:** "So about that," "Awesome," "you're welcome," "hate it when that happens."

Both channels use **ranking or journey frames** (tier list, iceberg, "three worlds, each stranger"). These give a built-in escalation and a reason to keep watching.

## 8. Steal / avoid

### Steal (1-min and 8-min)
1. **Give the narrator a character with a motive.** Sciencephile's narrator wants world domination; ours could be a smug guide or a doomed intern. Persona jokes are about 10% of their jokes and make everything else land.
2. **Every joke must use the fact it follows.** Test: if the joke can be moved to another video, cut it.
3. **Open with a concrete question or image, not a claim.** Then a curiosity gap ("there are three now"), then a promise of escalation.
4. **Put "you" physically inside the physics** (standing on it, falling in, holding a flashlight). Don't use "you" for life complaints.
5. **One test-dummy character per video** who suffers the consequences. That's perfect for stick figures.
6. **Earth anchor, then cosmic twist,** for every abstract fact. Translate every number into a household unit (bananas, cities, Europe-sized).
7. **Escalate:** order the facts small → big or normal → strange, and say so ("each stranger than the last").
8. **Rule of three with a wrong third item,** and the repeat-loop ("Black hole. Black hole. …Black hole.").
9. **Density targets:**
   - 1-min: 4–6 jokes, but the last 10–15 s is sincere awe.
   - 8-min: 2–3 jokes per minute, with real-science stretches of 30–45 s and no jokes.
10. **Mark delivery:** long beats around punch jokes, no beat for throwaways, `//` plus a short title line at each section change, and slow fragments for the awe ending.

### Avoid
1. **Relatable-life gags** (Mondays, traffic, Wi-Fi, bills). They don't explain anything and every channel uses them.
2. **Jokes after every line.** Even Sciencephile leaves 3–4 info lines between jokes, and the awe needs silence.
3. **Ending on a joke.** Both channels end on wonder; our "Wave hi, they're stuck in traffic" kills the awe.
4. **Roasting real nations or groups.** Sciencephile's Balkans and German-government jabs are its weakest, most dateable lines.
5. **Hedge-y meta lines that undercut the science** ("Science is mostly maybe, in a lab coat"). They make the claim seem pointless. Instead, state the doubt concretely (who says no, and why).

## 9. Demo rewrite (≈190 words, ≈65 s at 165 wpm)

```
Black holes have one rule. / Stuff goes in. // Nothing comes out. /
Not light. / Not information. / (fast) Not that sock you threw at one. //
So some physicists asked a rude question. //
(slow) What if we're already inside one? //
Let's test it. / You, / a spacesuit, / and a black hole the size of the universe. //
A small black hole would stretch you into spaghetti. /
But a really big one? / The pull is so smooth from head to toe / (fast) you'd cross the edge without spilling your tea. //
Now the fun part. / One idea says what falls in / doesn't crush to a point. //
It bounces. / Into a brand new universe, / expanding / from the inside. //
Which would look... / (slow) suspiciously like ours. //
And here's the creepy clue. / When astronomers sorted far-off galaxies, /
too many / spin the same way. // Like they all inherited one twist. //
Could be a fluke. / (fast) Astronomers are checking. The galaxies declined to comment. //
But if it's true, / every black hole up there / is a door. //
(slow) And on the other side, / somebody's sky. //
Maybe they're looking up too. //
(slow) Wondering / which side / is the inside.
```

**Why it follows the rubric:**
- The hook is "rule, then exception, then question," with the twist item "that sock."
- "You" is placed physically ("You, a spacesuit…").
- Every joke tests a fact: the tea shows the tidal forces, "declined to comment" shows the uncertainty, the sock shows that nothing escapes.
- The Kurzgesagt-style tonal close is sincere and has no joke.

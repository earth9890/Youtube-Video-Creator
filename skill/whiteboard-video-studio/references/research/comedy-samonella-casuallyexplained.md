# SamONellaAcademy and Casually Explained: how the comedy is built

**Corpus:** 6 SamONella transcripts (Misconceptions, Pirates, Tarrare, Inventions, Produce, Guiteau) and 5 Casually Explained ("CE") transcripts (English, Cooking, Food of the World, Healthy, Reddit). I read every transcript in full. All .clean.txt files are unpunctuated except CE "Healthy". The Guiteau transcript is missing two blocks of captions in the middle. Sponsor reads are left out of all counts. Counts are my own hand tally and could be about 15% off either way.

---

## 1. Hook (first ~30 s)

**SamONella, Pirates.** He calls the viewer by name ("hey little Jimmy"), and Jimmy answers in a fake dialogue. Next comes a question about a fantasy the kid likes. The narrator crushes it ("you couldn't be more wrong") and the kid swears in reaction. A nonsense rhyme chain (swashbuckle, corn shuckle…) inflates the fantasy before the thesis ("not all it's cracked up to be"). The roadmap is one line: "first we'll talk about the food."

**SamONella, Tarrare.** "Hey kids", then a shared disgust (competitive eating), then "imagine if…" upgrades a hobby into a lifelong curse. After a pause he says "introducing / Tarrare". The first absurd fact (a quarter of a cow a day) is followed straight away by its flip (he weighed 100 lb).

**SamONella, Produce.** He opens on a familiar movie trope (stranded on an island, bananas). A character cuts in with "they're made of lies". A naive question follows, then the narrator plays god: "I created you". Only then does the real premise arrive.

**CE, Reddit.** Size claim, then a credential that roasts himself (ten years of daily use "because I can't regulate my own behavior"). Then the promise ("a bit of a guide"), then a first tip that is helpful in tone and useless in content (the search bar is "purely for aesthetic purposes").

**CE, Healthy.** A pet peeve ("it's good for you") gets a fake translation ("it grows in nature"). An irony reverses it (we bred it that way). The myth (toxins) is pushed to an absurd consequence (Steve Irwin and a blueberry smoothie).

**Templates:**
- **SamONella:** Name the kid → ask about a fantasy they hold → crush it in one line → the kid reacts → thesis plus a one-line roadmap.
- **SamONella, variant:** Mild familiar disgust → "now imagine if it were *forced* on someone" → pause → "introducing [name]" → a huge fact → its contradiction.
- **CE:** Big claim → credential that undercuts the narrator → promise of a helpful guide → first tip, delivered straight and useless.

## 2. Joke engine taxonomy

The first number is SamONella, the second is CE. A joke that uses two mechanisms is counted under both, so the totals add up to more than the roughly 190 / 170 distinct jokes.

| # | Type | SamONella / CE | Evidence (short) | Template |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Character skit / fake dialogue** | 30 / 14 | "um excuse me sir I'm a vegan"; "Tarrare look at me did you eat a ___ baby" | [state fact] → [a stock character reacts to it as if it were today] → [a second voice, or the narrator, flattens them] |
| 2 | **Fake definition / "X is for Y"** | 6 / 50 | "mise en place… French for forgetting half the ___"; "unpopular opinion is for popular opinions" | [official term] → "which is [language] for" / "is for" → [the embarrassing truth of how people actually use it] |
| 3 | **Anticlimax / undercut** | 18 / 24 | "America is still figuring out what a kilometer is"; "a French tire company will finally rate your restaurant" | [grand build: goals, rankings, a list of serious items] → [last slot drops to something petty or literal] |
| 4 | **Narrator self-own / aside** | 14 / 16 | "I would draw what happened next but…channel deleted"; "not for me I ___ nailed it" | [general fact about people's failings] → "not me, obviously" → [the narrator fails at it on screen] |

| 11 | **Wordplay / literal reading** | 10 / 10 | "a horse to be beckoned… a force to be reckoned with"; "you can't get off, not figuratively" | [take an idiom literally, or a brand name at face value] → [explain it straight] |
| 12 | **Misdirection on the final word** | 6 / 8 | "insufferable people on the planet / customers"; "a diseased rat or the beard of your diseased crewmate" | [sentence points at an obvious target] → pause → [the target turns out to be the listener's own side] |
| 13 | **Escalation run** (multi-beat) | 8 runs / 4 runs | pirate night below deck; Tarrare eel → cat → corpses → baby; CE enthusiast shopping list | [beat 1: bad] → [beat 2: worse, same frame] → [beat 3: unthinkable] → [flat closing line] |
| 14 | **Callback** | 6 / 8 | "now the shirt is triple sad"; "quit bickering or no one gets tzatziki" (twice) | [joke A early] → [same words reused later on a new target, often +1 level] |
| 15 | **Visual completion** | 10 / 6 | "basically like a bunch of hairy unwashed…"; "eel skeleton looks like this" | [line stops early] → [the drawing finishes the joke] |
| 16 | **Shock or profanity punch** | 12 / 5 | bleeped words, sex jokes, gore | [clean sentence] → [one taboo word as the final stress] |

### How the two channels differ

**SamONella is a sketch engine.** Facts get dramatised. A historical person, a vegan, a used-car salesman or a narrator-god speaks in present-day slang inside the scene. Jokes come in chains of 2–4 tags on one fact. The vegan bit is three tags: the ask, "gluten-free", then "hemp rope". The narrator plays a crude older brother ("hey kids"), and the comedy runs on gross-out escalation and anachronism. The facts are left alone; they are funny because they are extreme, and he signals this with "and this is true" or "this is real."

**CE is a register engine.** The narrator never changes voice. He reads like a calm instruction manual: "nevertheless", "furthermore", "as an example", "the good news is". The joke is the gap between that formal register and petty content. His three workhorses are the fake definition, the fake advice and the anticlimax. A typical CE sentence is one line in two halves: "this won't do [the expected benefit] but it does [a petty social payoff]". Cooking alone uses it three times (honing rod, onion dicing, the $300 knife). CE's roasts aim at identity groups the viewer belongs to (Redditors, keto people, every nationality), so the viewer feels seen rather than lectured.

**Two mechanisms the Black Hole script needs most:**
- **The "good news" pivot (CE).** Myth → "in reality" correction → "*the good news is*" → absurd practical advice drawn from the correction. Example: too much alcohol is toxic, so pop a blueberry under your passed-out friend's tongue. It is a callback disguised as advice.
- **The god-narrator interruption (SamONella).** Characters can talk back to the narrator, and he punishes them ("you're next Susan… I created you"). This gives an explainer conflict without needing a plot.

## 3. Density and placement

**Measured in two videos:**
- **SamONella Pirates** (4.6 min, 1,034 words, no sponsor read): **~27 joke beats, about 5.9 per minute.** There are about 10 pure-information sentences, so info:joke is about **1 : 2.7**.
- **CE Cooking** (content portion ≈ 4.1 min, 942 words): **~27 joke beats, about 6.5 per minute.** Info:joke is about **1 : 3**. Almost every sentence ends in a punch. Fewer than 8 sentences are pure setup.

**Where jokes sit:**
- **CE:** at the **end of the same sentence that carries the fact**, usually after "which is", "but", or "this won't… but it will". The fact and the punch share one breath. The paragraph's last line is the hardest punch, then comes a long pause, then "nevertheless" resets.
- **SamONella:** in **tags after the fact.** The fact is stated straight, then 1–3 riffs or a skit follow. A second kind sits **mid-sentence**: brand names and slang dropped into the factual line ("you couldn't just snap into a Slim Jim").
- **Both:** a section **never ends on a fact.** It ends on a punch, and the next section opens on a fact.

**Longest stretches with no joke:**
- **Pirates:** the hardtack and scurvy explanations, about 83–84 words each (≈22 s). Each pays off hard at the end: weevils → vegan skit, and "causing you to die / so that's a lot of fun".
- **Cooking:** about 35 words (≈9 s).
- **Whole corpus:** CE "Healthy", the calories-in/out explanation, **133 words (≈40 s)**, closed by "say you wanted to get thick because it's 2019". Next is SamONella Guiteau's childhood trauma, 87 words (≈28 s), played straight for contrast.

**Rule they follow:** the silence ceiling is about 20–25 s. It stretches to 40 s only when the information is the useful payoff of the video, and a strong joke has to end it.

## 4. Story structure

**Tarrare (SamONella), a chronological biography with escalating horror:**

| % of runtime | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Hook: disgust → "imagine if" → "introducing" |
| 5–17 | Character sheet: appetite, weight, mouth, smell, each with a tag |
| 17–28 | Act 1: kicked out → street performer. Ends on a cliffhanger he refuses to draw ("let's just picture it for a few moments") as a re-hook |
| 28–34 | Hard section break ("moving on, cut to the year 1795"). Enlists; mock-hopeful line ("maybe that emptiness was a lack of purpose… turns out no") |
| 34–48 | Experiments escalate: feast → eel → cat. Verdict: "we got a goddamn demon on our hands" |
| 48–61 | **Twist:** the army turns the appetite into a weapon (a spy carrying a document in his belly) → captured, 30 hours on a latrine. Anticlimax: the note wasn't important |
| 61–79 | Decline escalation: dogs → blood → corpses → **missing baby (climax at 76%)** → death |
| 79–86 | Autopsy reveal (visual), then an **anti-moral** ending: "not even I can find anything resembling a moral" |
| 86–100 | Sponsor |

**Cooking (CE), a mock how-to that climbs the life cycle of a hobby:**

| % of runtime | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0–4 | Hook: three motives, last one broken |
| 4–15 | Beginner: Ramsay → an over-ambitious recipe → "serves two" |
| 15–39 | Prep steps (mise en place, knife, Instagram, smoke detector, wine as a "coping mechanism") |
| 39–51 | Plating and self-review, ending on "one star" |
| 51–71 | **Escalation:** "next level" shopping list. Onion technique → $300 knife → cast-iron fetish → sous-vide used once → broke → forearm tattoos "until the day you die" |
| 71–84 | Final question, "should I go pro?" Customers, frozen pizza, then the Michelin-is-a-tire-company **twist ending** |
| 84–100 | Sponsor, opened with a callback ("five Michelin stars") |

**Escalation in CE comes from identity stakes, not plot.** You go from someone who cooks to a person defined by cast iron. The Reddit video ends the same way: "you've been assimilated", then an identity checklist.

**Transitions:**
- **SamONella:** "first we'll talk about", "then there's", "finally there's", "moving on", "cut to", "introducing", "speaking of which", "anyway, back to whatever we were talking about", "matter of fact", "so yeah, in short".
- **CE:** "nevertheless" (×5 in Cooking), "relatedly", "as an example", "in reality", "the good news is", "now, at this point it's natural to ask", "ultimately".

**How a fact gets delivered:**
- **SamONella:** fact → tag(s) → a skit that re-enacts the fact. He sometimes pre-empts the viewer: "now for those of you who don't know, an eel skeleton looks like this."
- **CE:** **belief first** ("common knowledge says…") → "in reality" (the fact) → an absurd use of the fact. It is a myth–fact–misuse sandwich.

## 5. Sentence rhythm

**SamONella:**
- Long, breathless run-on sentences of 40–70 words chained with "and", "so" and "but", then a one- or two-word clipped tag: "whoops", "what a baller", "syphilis", "ears".
- "You know" appears 15 times; "like" 7.1 per 1,000 words. Slangy register ("hella", "wildin", "og") laid over textbook facts.
- Direct address to "kids" opens 3 of 6 videos.
- "You/your" is about 23 per 1,000 words; "I" is 6 per 1,000. The viewer is a character he talks *at*.

**CE:**
- Even, mid-length sentences of 15–30 words in manual-speak.
- "You/your/you're" is **40 per 1,000 words**; "I" is 14 per 1,000. Second-person instruction mixed with confession.
- Parallel lists are the backbone: "X is for Y" × ~30 in Reddit; breakfast / lunch / dinner / dessert; diet pros and cons.
- Rhetorical questions are put in the viewer's mouth ("now you might be saying…", "you might ask") and then corrected. "You might" appears 9 times.

**Both:** repetition with a change is how callbacks work (double sad → triple sad; "on white bread" ×4).

## 6. Delivery from the pause marks

**Speaking rate:**
- **SamONella:** 188–224 wpm, average ≈204. Long pauses: 0.9–5.5 per minute.
- **CE:** 190–228 wpm, average ≈207. Long pauses: 1.4–6.9 per minute.
- **The pattern across videos:** list or riff videos run fast with few pauses: Reddit (228 wpm, 1.4/min), Pirates (224, 1.3), Inventions (221, 1.5). Deadpan, one-item-at-a-time videos run slower with many pauses: Food (190, 6.9), English (194, 5.7), Misconceptions (190, 5.5).

**Ten examples (text before | text after):**

1. SamONella Misconceptions: "he / was just a guy named **//**" | "Gautama now **//** the shirt is triple sad". The pause comes **before** the reveal and again **before** the callback punch.
2. SamONella Misconceptions: "Hitler's elegant way of speaking **//**" | "or **//** the way Mussolini says spaghetti". A double beat **before** the broken last item.
3. SamONella Pirates: "wait a minute is this **//**" | "gluten-free". The pause comes **before** the punch word.
4. SamONella Pirates: "boy you look real pretty **//**" | "behind **//** oh wow / I'm boned". **Before** the punch, then **before** the reaction tag.
5. SamONella Tarrare: "Tarrare **//** look **//** at me did **//**" | "you eat a ___ baby". Stacked beats slow him down hard into the climax.
6. SamONella Inventions: "the wise men said big **//**" | "ears". The pause sits right before the last word.
7. CE Healthy: "they're like oh very **//**" | "disciplined when **//** in reality I just woke up at noon". **Before** the fake praise and **before** the reveal.
8. CE Cooking: "use it exactly one time **//**" | "run out of money". The pause comes **after** a punch, and the next punch follows bare.
9. CE Cooking: "all the food I make for myself would be **//**" | "nope frozen pizza and Jack Daniels". Before the punch.
10. CE Food: "pasta / pizza and gelato **//**" | "we hope that one day science may discover more". **After** the list, before the deadpan tag.
11. CE Food: "you've heard of cheesy potatoes **//**" | "but here comes the remix **//**". Before the reveal, and after it for the picture.
12. Section changes: SamONella "cut to the year 1795 **//** the **//** war…"; CE "…until the day you die **//**" | "at this point… should I go pro". A long pause marks the new section.

**What this adds up to:**
- **SamONella pauses *before* the punch,** mostly inside skits: a beat before the character's line, then the punch. **After** a punch he usually **doesn't pause at all.** "Rinse and repeat for 8 hours and then it's daytime" runs straight into "the poor diet…". The gore list (shoot, kick, chop, gouge, wind intestines) has **no** pause marks across about 40 words, so the escalation is rushed and the stop is abrupt. This is fast deadpan, and the drawings carry the laugh.
- **CE pauses *after* the punch:** about 7 of the 10 content `//` marks in Cooking come right after a punchline. That is silent laugh room, and the narrator doesn't laugh. A short `/` before the punch word is the norm ("insufferable people on the planet / customers"). His lists of mistakes or items run fast with no marks; the verdict line after them gets the long beat.
- **Where the transcript drops the punch word:** "beef ___ //", "serves two //", "three ___ //". The ASR often loses the punch word itself. The punch was probably said quietly or under music. Don't push the volume up on the punch: say it flat, then hold the silence.
- **Do they speed up for asides?** Yes. Asides and bracketed jabs run with no marks: "who was in it? ___ everyone. anyway", and "for some reason the community is also really into crypto". Emphasis lines and reveals get broken up with slashes.

## 7. Signature moves

**SamONella:**
1. "Hey kids" plus an older-brother menace toward the viewer.
2. Characters argue with the narrator, and the narrator punishes them.
3. Historical people speak in present-day slang.
4. Gross-out escalation ladders with a calm sign-off.
5. "This is real" or "and this is true" to vouch for the absurd fact.
6. Refusing a moral, or refusing to draw something.

**CE:**
7. Manual-voice connectors ("nevertheless", "relatedly", "the good news is").
8. Fake definitions and "X is for Y" lists.
9. "This won't [benefit] but it will [petty payoff]".
10. Self-own asides ("not for me, I nailed it", then he fails), and roasting the viewer's own tribe.

## 8. Steal / avoid

**Steal:**
1. **One sentence = fact + punch.** Put the joke in the last clause of the fact sentence, not in a separate gag line.
2. **Aim for 5–6 joke beats a minute.** Never run more than about 20 s without one. The only exception is the one core explanation, and a strong punch must close it.
3. **Give the narrator a character** (a weary guide, a menacing older brother, a smug god) and keep that voice the whole way.
4. **Let a stick figure talk back,** and have the narrator flatten it.
5. **Use the "good news" pivot:** myth → "in reality" → "the good news is" → absurd advice built from the real fact.
6. **Break the last item of every list** with a petty motive, a literal reading, or a self-own.
7. **Be specific:** pick one exact, strange object instead of a category.
8. **Escalate in 3+ beats on one frame,** then end on a flat line ("so that's a lot of fun").
9. **Set up a callback** early and pay it off in the last line, raised one level.
10. **Timing for a TTS voice:** `/` before the punch word, `//` after the punch, then a hard reset word ("Anyway." / "Nevertheless."). Run lists and asides with no pauses.

**Avoid:**
1. **Bolted-on "relatable" gags** (Mondays, traffic, Wi-Fi, WhatsApp groups) that have nothing to do with the fact. Every joke above comes out of its own fact.
2. **Explaining the joke, or laughing at it** ("Surprise! It's us"). Deadpan only.
3. **A string of unrelated one-liners.** Without escalation or callbacks there's no shape.
4. **Ending a section on a bare fact.**
5. **Sound effects like "Boom!" in place of a punchline.**

## 9. Demo rewrite (~180 words, ≈60 s)

Narrator: weary SamONella-style older brother, with CE manual-voice lines.

> Hey kids. // Look around your room. / Bed. / Desk. / That one sock you've been ignoring since March.
> Now (slow) what if the whole universe / is inside a black hole?
> (fast) Not a metaphor. Actual physicists with actual tenure think this.
> Quick recap: a black hole is a star that died so hard / it folded space shut. // Anything that falls in / stays in. Light. / Planets. / Kevin, / who leaned over the edge for a selfie. //
> So / if we fell in, / would we feel it?
> (slow) No. //
> (fast) A hole big enough to hold a universe stretches you so gently you'd cross the edge, look around, and go: / "Huh. / Thought it'd be more." //
> The evidence: astronomers checked a few hundred galaxies, and about two thirds spin the same way. / Like the universe was born on a playground roundabout / and nobody ever let it off. //
> Other scientists looked at the same data and said / "eh." // Which, in science, / is a full rebuttal.
> But if it's true, / every black hole out there is somebody's entire universe. (slow) Somewhere, / a little stick guy is hearing this exact fact. //
> (fast) He also hasn't moved his sock.

Mechanisms used:
- **List with a broken last item:** the room objects (ends on the sock) and the things that fall in (ends on Kevin).
- **Absurd specificity:** Kevin's selfie.
- **Fake dialogue plus undercut:** "Huh. Thought it'd be more."
- **Modern-mundane comparison:** the playground roundabout.
- **Fake definition:** "eh" counting as a full rebuttal in science.
- **Callback raised one level:** the sock returns, now in a universe inside a black hole.

The facts stay accurate:
- Tidal forces at the edge of a supermassive black hole are gentle.
- The galaxy-spin claim refers to the JWST deep-field rotation study, where roughly two thirds of a few hundred galaxies spin the same way.

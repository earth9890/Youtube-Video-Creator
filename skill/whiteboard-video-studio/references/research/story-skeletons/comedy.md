# Story skeletons: comedy (SamONella Academy, Casually Explained)

This file covers 11 videos: 6 SamONella and 5 Casually Explained (CE). Timestamps come from the `en-orig.vtt` word timings, because the `.timed.txt` files carry no `[mm:ss]` stamps. They were rebuilt into roughly 8 s chunks, so each one is accurate to about ±8 s. The Guiteau captions are missing 6:06-7:06. All lines are paraphrased.
Data: `data/comedy.json`. The joke mechanics, hooks and delivery for these channels are covered in `../comedy-samonella-casuallyexplained.md` and are not repeated here.

**The short answer.** These channels joke every few seconds and still feel like stories because **the joke sits on top of a beat that moves**. Every item is a reversal ("you think X, BUT"), and the strongest videos chain their sections with cause and effect ("bad food and cramped bunks, THEREFORE disease"). When a list has no protagonist and no reversals, it falls back into the "fact, joke, AND, fact" essay. The lowest-viewed video in the group (Food of the World, 6.4M) is exactly that.

### SamONellaAcademy — Historical Misconceptions For You to Bring Up during Family Dinner (23.7M, 5.7 min; 4.5 min before the sponsor)
- **Story engine:** other: myth-bust countdown, framed as a mission.
- **Protagonist:** the narrator, "ordered from upstairs" to clean up the myths. The viewer is the culprit who spreads them. Want: purge ten myths.
- **Central question:** which ten things you believe are false? Planted at 0:21. The countdown closes at 4:21 (76% of runtime, 97% of the content).
- **Stakes:** you embarrass yourself at family dinner (from the title). They are low and social, and that is enough.
- **Obstacle:** a wrong belief, one per item.
- **Beat chain:**
  - 0:12 AND: the narrator is ordered to clear up ten myths.
  - 0:21 BUT: nobody burned at Salem; the real death (pressed under stones, still asking for "more weight") beats the myth.
  - 1:03 AND (inner BUT): the fat Buddha is a different figure, and Buddha was a man. A shirt gag gets sadder each time.
  - 1:20 AND (inner BUT): the vomitorium was an exit.
  - 1:28 AND (inner BUT): the cherry tree, with a skit on why the story makes no sense.
  - 1:54 AND (inner BUT): pyramid builders were paid workers.
  - 2:20 AND (inner BUT): the Great Wall can't be seen from space.
  - 2:54 AND (inner BUT): the dictators didn't build the roads, so you have nothing left to like about them.
  - 3:30 AND (inner BUT): iron maidens were a Victorian fake.
  - 4:04 AND (inner BUT): Einstein never failed maths.
  - 4:21 SO: we all have more to learn, which bridges into the sponsor.
- **Open loops:** "who was squished to death?" (0:29, paid 0:54). "Was he a witch?" is left open on purpose (0:54). The shirt runner runs 1:03 to 1:20. Wall width vs space height runs 2:20 to 2:37. A new mini-question about every 34 s (median), always at the top of an item.
- **Turns:** the truth is better than the myth (0:29). The workers had better perks than astronauts (1:54). The last good thing about the dictators is taken away (3:03).
- **Ending:** the countdown completes, then a soft moral and the sponsor. There is no callback to the opening frame. Order matters: the most dramatic true story comes first and the myth that flatters the viewer most comes last (the "I failed maths like Einstein" crowd).
- **Where facts sit:** each fact answers "is the thing you believe true?" Examples: the wall is about 30 m wide and space starts 100 km up, so it's like naming the colour of a guitar pick across a football field. The Salem fact becomes "how did the one man die?" and the answer is the stones.
- **Where jokes sit:** on every item, as the tag after the reversal. Skits (Tyler and the tree, Cornelius and the maiden, Dmitri) dramatise the myth's origin, so they are still on-beat.
- **Transplant template:** *[Narrator] is ordered to fix the [N] lies [viewer] keeps repeating. Lie 1: [the most dramatic one; the truth is a better story]. Lies 2..N-1: [belief] BUT [reality], each with one image analogy. Lie N: [the myth that flatters the viewer most], taken away. SO: you have more to learn.*

### SamONellaAcademy — Tarrare, the Hungriest Man in History (20.6M, 7.8 min; 6.6 before the sponsor)
- **Story engine:** real-person discovery, a rise-and-fall biography.
- **Protagonist:** Tarrare. Want: to satisfy his hunger, and from 4:50 to be cured.
- **Central question:** what happens to a man whose biology forces him to eat like a competitive eater forever? Planted 0:15-0:24. Answered by his death and autopsy at 5:59-6:24 (78%).
- **Stakes:** his life, and later other people's (the missing child).
- **Obstacle:** his own body (nature), and a society that keeps expelling him.
- **Beat chain:**
  - 0:24 AND: born poor, eats a quarter cow a day.
  - 0:41 BUT: he weighs about 100 lb, with a freak mouth and a stench.
  - 1:15 THEREFORE: his family throws him out.
  - 1:32 THEREFORE: he begs, then eats anything for crowds as a street act.
  - 1:57 BUT: a blockage puts him in hospital on laxatives.
  - 2:18 THEREFORE: he enlists looking for purpose, BUT quadruple rations still aren't enough.
  - 2:35 THEREFORE: doctors experiment on him (a feast for 15, an eel, a cat).
  - 3:34 SO: verdict, demon.
  - 3:42 THEREFORE: the army weaponises him as a spy who swallows the message.
  - 4:08 BUT: he speaks no German and eats trash in public, so he is caught.
  - 4:31 BUT: after 30 hours chained to a latrine, the note turns out to be worthless.
  - 4:50 THEREFORE: he begs for a cure, BUT nothing works, and he escalates to dogs, blood and corpses.
  - 5:24 BUT: a toddler vanishes and he is expelled.
  - 5:59 THEREFORE: he dies of TB; the autopsy shows a body built around food.
  - 6:24 SO: anti-moral.
- **Open loops (11):** "obese?" is answered instantly (0:41). "What happened after the laxatives?" is refused on purpose (1:57), which works as a re-hook. "Can he deliver a box?" runs 3:51 to 3:59. "Will the spy mission work?" runs 4:08 to 4:25. "What was the secret note?" runs 4:34 to 4:42. "Can he be cured?" runs 4:50 to 6:07. A new question every 26 s (median).
- **Turns:** thin, not fat (0:41). The search for purpose fails at once (2:27). **The curse becomes an asset** (3:42), which is the midpoint reversal at about 47% of the content. The note was worthless (4:42), an anticlimax used as a reversal. The missing baby (5:41) is the climax at 85% of the content.
- **Ending:** death, then an autopsy picture whose pie chart (90% food) echoes his mind, then "no moral here". The anti-moral itself is a callback to the "ask what it means" habit of explainers.
- **Where facts sit:** "what did the doctors do with him?" is answered by the experiment list. "How do you smuggle a document through enemy land?" is answered by "he swallowed it, and it came out two days later".
- **Where jokes sit:** almost all on story beats. The family-insult skit sits on the expulsion, the general skit on the weaponisation, the doctor skit on the baby. One aside (the "frog" racism bit) is a digression inside the expulsion beat.
- **Transplant template:** *[Person] is born with [extreme trait]. You'd expect [obvious consequence] BUT [stranger reality]. THEREFORE [first rejection]. THEREFORE [makes a living from the trait] BUT [the trait backfires]. THEREFORE [joins a bigger system for purpose] BUT [the trait still wins]. THEREFORE [the system studies, then weaponises the trait] BUT [the trait sabotages the mission]. THEREFORE [seeks a cure] BUT [worst escalation]. THEREFORE [end] SO [anti-moral or twisted moral].*

BUT one upside: [fake relief]. SO surely [the romantic part] is great? BUT it's the worst part. THEREFORE [the famous fictional hero] would die. SO stay home.*

### Shape of the short videos (under about 5 min of content: Pirate 4.6, Misconceptions 4.5, English 4.8, Cooking 4.0)
1. **0-10%:** the protagonist's belief or desire, in one line, from a named stand-in (Jimmy, "you", the smug narrator). It is denied or complicated by 0:25.
2. **10-65%:** 3-4 sections of about 45-60 s, each opening its own question, ordered by escalation (food, then crowding, then disease, then combat; first try, then routine, then obsession, then pro). Pirate and Cooking link sections causally ("bad food and cramped bunks, therefore disease"; "lesson learned, therefore an easier recipe").
3. **65-80%: the last-hope rung** in the viewer's voice: "surely the battles are fun?" (Pirate 3:15), "should I go pro?" (Cooking 3:15). Reddit (5:38, "what's my karma for?") and Food (7:12) do the same in longer form. **4 of 11 videos use this beat; 2 of the 4 short ones do.**
4. **80-95%:** crush the last hope, then end within 20 s on a callback to the hook (Jack Sparrow ↔ the movies; Michelin ↔ "serves two" and the five-star review; the accent ↔ "every accent has an advantage").
5. **No re-introductions, and no "and also" sections after the crush.**

**Endings:** 7 of 11 pay off the hook's frame (Pirate, Guiteau, Tarrare, Cooking, Reddit, English, Food). 3 of those are monkey's-paw endings, where the want is granted in a twisted form: Guiteau's fame via the gallows, Reddit karma as assimilation, the chef's prize from a tyre company. The 4 that drop the frame (Misconceptions, Inventions, Produce, Healthy) end on a soft moral that runs into the ad.

### How a 1-minute version compresses the skeleton
What survives, using Pirate as the model (about 150 words):
1. **Want plus denial (0-8 s):** "You want to be a pirate? Bad news."
2. **Rung 1, an everyday misery (8-22 s):** one fact and one joke (weevil bread eaten in the dark).
3. **Rung 2, BECAUSE of rung 1 (22-36 s):** the consequence (scurvy, stage by stage, in 3 quick lines).
4. **Last hope (36-44 s):** "but at least the sword fights are cool, right?"
5. **Crush plus callback sting (44-60 s):** the real fight is a massacre, so the movie hero dies in 2 seconds.

Cut: the catalogue middle, all digressions, every second skit, the soft moral. A biography compresses the same way: trait, then rejection (BECAUSE trait), then the trait becomes an asset, then the asset backfires, then a twisted ending. **The beats that survive are the BUT and THEREFORE beats. Every AND beat is cut first.**

### 5 rules a writer can apply tomorrow
1. **Give the facts a protagonist who wants something, even if it's just "you" or a kid named Jimmy, so that each fact becomes an obstacle.**
   - Evidence: the 5 want-driven videos (Tarrare, Guiteau, Pirate, Cooking, Reddit) are 82% causal links. The 6 lists and catalogues are 33%.
   - Pirate turns a "facts about pirates" list into "Jimmy's dream, dismantled".
2. **If it must be a list, every item must flip a belief ("you think X, BUT Y").** The joke goes on the flip, not beside it.
   - Evidence: Misconceptions, the group's top video at 23.7M, is all AND links but 82% reversals. Food (6.4M) has a 33% reversal density, the lowest.
3. **Chain sections with BECAUSE, not "and next".** Make each section's fact the cause of the next section's problem.
   - Evidence: "the poor diet and cramped conditions led to disease" (Pirate 2:32). "Lesson learned, so an easier recipe" (Cooking 0:21). Tarrare's "the curse becomes a weapon, the weapon fails, he seeks a cure".
   - The top 4 run 60% BUT/THEREFORE; the rest run 19%.
4. **Open a new question every ~35 s, and hold the central one open to about 75-80% of runtime.** Before the end, plant a last-hope question in the viewer's voice and crush it.
   - Evidence: median loop gap 34.5 s. Central loop closed at a median 78%. The last-hope beat appears in 4 videos (Pirate 3:15, Cooking 3:15, Reddit 5:38, Food 7:12).
   - The slowest cadence (Food, 95 s) is the lowest-viewed video.
5. **End by paying off the hook. The best version grants the want in a twisted form.** Never drop the opening frame.
   - Evidence: 7/11 close the frame. The monkey's-paw endings (Guiteau gets fame by hanging; karma buys assimilation; the chef's prize comes from a tyre company) are the most quotable.
   - Produce opens on stranded castaways and never returns to them; it is the lowest-viewed SamONella video (17.7M).

**How the jokes coexist with the spine:** in the story-shaped videos, skits sit on turning points, not beside them. The general recruiting Tarrare is the weaponise beat, the doctor's "did you eat a baby" is the climax, and the stalking montage is Guiteau's last attempt. Digressions (Grimace lore, leaded petrol, the frog aside) are short (10-20 s) and always end with an explicit return ("anyway, back to…", "moving on"). One long digression is allowed if it sets up a later reversal: the commune's rules pay off when the commune rejects him.
